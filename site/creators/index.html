What PadSpan is

PadSpan is a Home Assistant integration that shows which room every phone, watch and tag is in, live, on a plan of your home, using the Bluetooth proxies you already have. The core is free and open source. PadSpan Pro places every light and device where it really is on the Atlas, PadSpan's map of the whole house, and adds Live Aboard, which shows the same house in 3D to turn and zoom the way you'd look into a house in The Sims.

Record the demo

The demo is the real PadSpan panel running in your browser on a sample house laid out like a real 5,000 sq ft test home, with three furnished floors (the top one has a loft over the garage) and two residents and a guest moving between the rooms. There's nothing to install, and you can screen-record it at any size. It runs with PadSpan Pro switched on, so Live Aboard, the wall screen and the placed lights can all be shown.

The facts

NamePadSpan, one word with a capital S. The integration is PadSpan HA, and the paid key is PadSpan Pro.
What it doesWorks out which room each phone, watch, tag and beacon is in, every 5 seconds, from what your Bluetooth proxies hear, and shows it on a plan of your home, one floor at a time or all floors stacked. Your automations get room, distance and home/away sensors.
What you needHome Assistant 2024.1 or newer and at least one Bluetooth scanner: an ESPHome Bluetooth proxy, a Shelly Gen2 or newer set as a Bluetooth scanner, or your Home Assistant machine's own Bluetooth. One tells you home or away; one in each room you want told apart gives you rooms. A floor plan is optional, because rooms come from each scanner's area in Home Assistant.
InstallIt's in the HACS default list, and one click opens it in HACS. From Home Assistant 2026.11 (out in November), the same link opens it in Settings → Marketplace.
FreeRoom presence and its sensors, floor plans and rooms, calibration, the coverage tools, and the Atlas with your floors and rooms and a tap-to-toggle marker for each light in its room. Free and open source under GPL-3.0.
PadSpan ProEvery light and device placed exactly on the Atlas, Live Aboard (the 3D house, still experimental) with its furniture, the wall screen, Forensics, Busy Times and Locate. Anyone can try all of Pro free for 90 days, no card: one trial per home, with the key sent by email. From the 0.38.180 beta, the trial also needs the usage report on until it ends. Keys and prices are on padspan.traks.ca.
BermudaPadSpan doesn't need Bermuda. If you already run it, keep it: PadSpan runs next to it on the same proxies. See Bermuda and PadSpan together.
PrivacyIt runs on your own Home Assistant and works offline. It checks for updates once a day, which you can turn off, and with a key it checks the licence once a day. Opening the Objects list can ask macvendors.com and maclookup.app which company made up to 40 listed devices. It sends only the first three bytes of an address, and only after a Bluetooth receiver has confirmed that the device has a fixed, maker-assigned address (ESPHome proxies on version 2022.12 or newer and Home Assistant's own Bluetooth say so, and PadSpan remembers it; Shelly, ESPresense and Tasmota don't, so a device only they have heard isn't looked up). The usage report is opt-in and off by default.
Who makes itGarry Broeckling. Architecture, testing and releases are human-directed, and the implementation is AI-assisted using Claude (how it's built).
MoreThe GitHub repo and README, with a comparison table, and the guides, such as Bermuda, ESPresense or PadSpan?

Good to know before you record

  • The demo and its pictures come from recent builds, and everything they show is in the 0.38.199 stable release.
  • The demo has PadSpan Pro switched on, with Live Aboard, Show people and Show tags & scanners turned on. In a new install, the Pro parts need a key or the 90-day trial, and those three stay off until you turn them on. Without the last two, the Atlas and the wall show no people or tags.
  • Forensics, Busy Times and Traceback's movement playback have no data in the demo, so show those on a real install.
  • The panel is in English.
