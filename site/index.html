What's new

The releases since v0.21, in plain terms

If you installed PadSpan earlier this year and left it alone, a lot has changed underneath — and there is a reason you may not have heard about any of it. See the full changelog for every entry.

If you are on the stable channel and think you are current, check again. The update check built into the integration reported v0.21.13 as the latest stable right up until 23 August 2026, long after newer stable releases existed. That was a fault at my end, not at yours. It is fixed, and cutting a release now publishes the version manifest as part of the process, so it cannot drift like that again.
v0.38.38 Stable 11 September 2026
  • Changed: everything in 0.38.37 below, promoted from beta to stable so HACS's default channel picks up the trial — no code changes since.
v0.38.37 Beta channel 11 September 2026
  • Added: an install with no licence key gets a Start 3-month free trial button in Settings → Features → PadSpan licence — one email, no payment info, no reinstall. It mints a real trial key through the same licence-server path a purchase uses, so it gates identically: expiry, the 14-day grace window and the editing-only gate (nothing already placed is ever lost) all apply exactly as they do for a paid key, with no separate logic anywhere. One redo is allowed if it's needed again (matched on email or install id, so rotating just one of the two doesn't reset it); the licence server also throttles by requesting IP so a script can't mint a stream of fabricated identities. The free-lighting paywall banner and the tiers wizard both now point at it instead of asserting no trial exists.
  • Added: opt-in telemetry now includes up to 10 of an install's saved Showcase preset *values* (never the preset's own name), giving a future top-10 pulldown real data to learn from.
  • Fixed: the Lights map's floor index used to draw one row per floor, so a multi-storey house grew a legend taller than the map itself — every floor now shares one line, and the canvas height shrinks to the real footprint instead of always reserving the worst-case size.
v0.38.36 Stable 11 September 2026

At least one real sensor (a HOBEIAN vibration sensor doing duty as "Upper Garage Car Door Contact") reports backwards — state=="on" means closed. Also fixed: saving a preset immediately after changing a Showcase/Automorph control, before that change's own save finished, could capture the value from *before* the change instead of what was on screen. Neither had a test before today; both do now, alongside 10 more tests for preset validation that also had none.

v0.38.7 – v0.38.3125 August – 10 September 2026

Everything below this line was shipped one release at a time and never made it up here as it happened — 25 releases' worth, grouped by what actually changed rather than listed one by one. The full changelog has the day-by-day version.

  • Door and window sensors — and now locks — link onto the actual wall on the map, not a settings tab. What began as a Rooms → RF Barriers pick-a-wall step moved onto the Lights map itself, then a genuinely unusable "click the wall, then its two ends" picker was rebuilt from scratch into place-and-drag: drop a circle on the opening, resize it, and the two points where it crosses a wall become the edges. Locks got the same tool for their own wall section, reading as a plain closed wall when locked and flashing red when not. Committing an opening now goes through the same Save bar every other draft edit on the map already uses, and a real sizing bug in the picker's nearest-wall math was found and fixed along the way. In the same push, the Overview coverage heatmap stopped being a mosaic of hatch-textured cells that read as noise and became a continuous, perceptually-ramped raster, and a wall on one storey stopped drawing itself onto the storey above or below at that storey's height.
  • PadSpan Bright is public, and setup finally has a guide. Bright now has its own HACS repository, built and published automatically from the same source as PadSpan HA on every release, so it cannot drift out of sync by hand. Three guided wizards — Mapping, the Lights builder, and Calibration — walk through the parts of setup that used to be a bare checklist, and a new tier wizard explains in plain terms what Free, Bright, Bright Pro and Pro each actually unlock. Along the way, every one of the 44 in-app help entries was checked against the real code; 22 had drifted from what the product actually does.
  • Fans, motion sensors and temperature sensors joined lights as first-class devices on the map, each with their own code series, glyph, and — where it makes sense — controls, alongside a full redesign of how a tap or hold on the map actually works: a short tap switches, a hold opens controls, a room or floor name opens an "everything there" sheet, and a pinch-to-zoom, one-finger-pan gesture pipeline replaced the old fixed view. A motion sensor now glows for up to six hours after it last tripped, sweeping blue to violet as a visible sense of how long ago instead of a number you have to go look up.
  • Showcase learned to render light instead of a coloured dot, tinting white bulbs from their real Kelvin temperature, clipping each pool to the room it is actually in, and shaping the beam by fixture type — plus spatial scenes, Ripple, and Isolux illuminance contours, all sampled from the same real-metres model the map itself uses. Automorph, the ambient aura mode, grew from 3 styles to 11.
  • The occupancy number counts people now, not things with a name. "7 identified" used to mean a key fob, a truck beacon counted twice, and a box — with the one actual person in the room sometimes not counted at all, because a rotating BLE address was dropped before it ever reached the estimator. It is rebuilt around Home Assistant's own person entities, a rotating-address heuristic for the phones and watches nobody has claimed, and occupancy/presence sensors read as a floor under the count rather than something that adds to it — with an honest low/high range instead of a single confident-looking number that was not. A related pass fixed several coordinate bugs from issue #62: rooms that stayed the wrong size after a later map correction, and a room trace that did not follow an image crop the way everything else on the map does.
  • The 3D map stopped wasting most of the panel it was drawn in. Two separate fixed-size margins were stacking on top of each other, so the map filled barely 70% of its own panel on a compact screen — which is also why beacons had looked oversized: the browser was zooming in to compensate for the wasted space. Both margins are proportional now, the map fills about 93%, and beacon labels no longer eat a quarter of the width on a small screen either.
v0.38.6 Beta channel 25 August 2026
  • An established install is no longer told it has not started. v0.38.5 briefly flashed “Setup Progress — 0/5 — Upload Floor Plan” at settled installs every time the panel opened. Nothing was wrong with them: the check read the map list before it had been fetched, and an empty list was mistaken for an unfinished setup. It mattered most on that release of all releases, because 0.38.5 is the one that rewrote how every map stores its position — being told you had never uploaded a floor plan invited the conclusion that the upgrade had eaten your setup. An empty list is an answer; an unfetched one is not.
  • A radio that is transmitting now says so. The inner ring of a radio’s map marker turns red and blinks while that radio is actively scanning, on both the 3D house view and the flat map, and the hover text names the mode. Only a confirmed active is coloured — a radio on auto, or one that does not report its mode at all, is left alone, because not transmitting and not saying are different facts.
  • If your ESPHome YAML says active: true and PadSpan shows passive, the YAML is not what decides any more. Home Assistant 2026.6 changed the default scanning mode for every ESPHome Bluetooth proxy to auto, and it overrides what the device’s own firmware config asks for. Reflashing cannot change this. The setting that does decide is per device, under Settings → Devices & Services → ESPHome → your device → Configure → Bluetooth scanning mode. There is no fleet-wide switch. Worth knowing before you change it: active scanning means the radio transmits, which costs 2.4 GHz airtime you may be sharing with Wi-Fi.
v0.38.5 Stable 25 August 2026
  • Everything below this line reached everyone. Stable moved from v0.37.0 straight to v0.38.5, so the whole 0.38 series — placement stored once in metres, the licence card, the what's-new card, and PadSpan Bright Pro — arrives in a single update.
  • Upgrading from 0.37? Nothing to do. The placement conversion takes an automatic snapshot before it changes anything and refuses to run at all if that snapshot cannot be taken. If something ever looks wrong, restore it from inside PadSpan under Health → Backup/Restore — not by rolling back in HACS, which would leave you on an older version reading converted files.

v0.38.0 Beta channel 25 August 2026

A map’s placement is now stored once, in metres. Four bugs that looked separate turned out to be the same one, and this removes the room they lived in rather than patching each.

  • Trimming, re-aligning or re-mastering a plan can no longer skew the house. A map’s position used to be written down twice, in two different units, and any operation that updated one copy and not the other left the two disagreeing. There is one copy now, so that disagreement has nowhere to exist.
  • Two live errors died with it. Calibration and Traceback were applying a map’s horizontal scale to the vertical axis, which on a trimmed reference map drew an object up to ten metres from where it actually was. Nothing in the interface showed that anything was wrong.
  • Point Align stops squashing a map onto a differently-shaped one (v0.37.1). Aligning a plan onto a map with a different aspect ratio came out compressed by exactly the ratio between the two pictures.
  • Plans can be genuinely skewed, not just moved and scaled. A scan that is slightly sheared — a photograph of a paper plan, most often — can now be described exactly instead of approximately.
  • There is no “master map” to lose any more. The star that marked one plan as the anchor for all the others is gone; every map now carries its own position in metres, so nothing breaks when it is changed.
  • Restoring a backup no longer writes one map’s alignment onto another. Restores matched maps by name, so a backup containing “Main Floor” would overwrite the alignment of any existing map with that name.

On the beta channel while the conversion is proven on real installs. It rewrites how every map records its position, so it takes a backup of every store before it starts and marks each map as it goes, so an interrupted run can be resumed rather than half-applied.

Why PadSpan

A complete tracking workstation, not just a sensor

Most BLE presence integrations give you a config flow and a sensor. PadSpan gives you a floor plan editor, room boundary polygons, 3D isometric maps, walk-around calibration, follow mode with email alerts, a training hub, and a full sample/demo mode — so you can explore every feature before plugging in hardware.

It works with the BLE scanners you already have. Home Assistant Bluetooth proxies and Bermuda proxies are picked up directly. ESPresense nodes arrive over MQTT, which you switch on in Manage → ESPresense MQTT and which needs Home Assistant's MQTT integration configured. No custom firmware. No cloud dependency. Everything runs locally.

Lighting

Your lights on the same floor plan

PadSpan already knows the shape of your house in metres — the rooms, the floors, the walls. Mapping → Lights puts your Home Assistant lights, fans, motion and temperature sensors, locks, and doors and windows on that same plan, so you can see and switch them where they actually are instead of hunting through a list of entity names.

Free, in every install No key

  • One marker per light, in the room it belongs to

Phone & Watch Tracking

Phones rotate their Bluetooth address every ~15 minutes specifically to prevent tracking. PadSpan offers multiple paths depending on your device:

Installation

Up and running in minutes

Requirements: Home Assistant 2024.1+, at least one BLE scanner (ESPresense, Bermuda proxy, or HA Bluetooth proxy), and HACS (recommended).

Via HACS (recommended)

  1. Open HACS in your Home Assistant instance
  2. Add gbroeckling/padspanHA as a custom repository (Integration type)
  3. Search for and install PadSpan HA
  4. Restart Home Assistant completely
  5. Add the integration: Settings → Devices & Services → Add Integration → PadSpan HA

Quick Start

  1. Open the PadSpan HA panel in the sidebar
  2. The onboarding wizard guides you: upload a map, set scale, draw rooms, place scanners, calibrate
  3. Try Sample mode to explore every feature with demo data

PadSpan Pro

Pro features, one key — the core stays free

PadSpan HA is and remains free, open-source (GPL) software. Pro is an optional license that unlocks advanced features inside the same integration — no separate install, no cloud dependency, all data stays on your Home Assistant instance.

What Pro unlocks today

  • Forensics — search which BLE devices were near your scanners in any time window. Recorded presence sessions (dwell time, peak signal, per-scanner detail) plus lower-confidence "possible" matches, with CSV export.
  • Opt-in and privacy-guarded: off by default, explicit enable confirmation, configurable retention (7–90 days), recordings never leave your instance.
  • Light placement — place each light exactly where it hangs, with fixture shapes and sizes, WLED support, Showcase and Fit room. See Lighting.
  • Everything in PadSpan Bright Pro is included. Pro is the whole product; Bright Pro is its lighting half sold separately. Never buy both.
  • More Pro features will join under the same key.
