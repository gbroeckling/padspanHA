Control the house from the map

Five add-ons for running the house from the Atlas: the music, the alarm and the garage door, the heating, the lights, and the whole house at once.

Audio

From the hallway screen, pick what the kitchen plays, turn it down, skip the track or send it to the deck too. Press Room off as you leave.

What you get

  • A speaker beside a room's name on the Atlas while one of its players is on, struck through when it's muted. Tap it, or the room's name, to open the room's card.
  • Now playing for each player in the room's card: the cover, the title and the artist, with play and pause, previous and next, and stop. A Control4 room also gets rewind and fast-forward where its Home Assistant integration offers them.
  • Volume: a slider for a player that takes a level, or − and + for one that steps it, as Control4's do. Mute, On and ⏻.
  • Source: the input, service or favourite to play, from a row of buttons or, for a long list, a list you can search. Mode: a receiver's or soundbar's sound mode, with Denon's long list in groups (Direct, Stereo, Movie, music, game, Decoders), and what it is playing now.
  • Sound: the tone and surround settings the player's own Home Assistant integration offers, and only those: bass, treble, balance, loudness, EQ, the subwoofer, surround level and mode, speech enhancement and dialogue, night mode, lip sync, and Denon's Dynamic EQ. Sonos, Russound, Yamaha MusicCast, Music Assistant, Xantech and Nuvo players have them.
  • Share: play this room's music in other rooms too, each with its own volume, and leave the group again. For players that group in Home Assistant (Sonos, Music Assistant, HEOS, MusicCast, WiiM, Bluesound and others), with players from the same system.
  • Room off, first in the card, turns off the room's lights and players that are on in one press. All audio off turns off the music in every room, after asking.
  • Match rooms: an administrator picks the room each player is in, once. PadSpan guesses first from the player's Home Assistant area or its name. Several players can share a room, a Control4 zone and a Denon zone, say. On the same card: Sources, to rename, hide and order each player's sources (Home Assistant can't), and a Volume limit for any player, kept everywhere its volume is set.
  • Include Home Assistant speakers, on the matching card, adds Music Assistant, ESPHome, Cast and TV players. It's off unless you tick it.

Where. The Atlas page. On a wall screen, and with PadSpan Pro's new layout, each screen ticks Audio in its ⋯ list. Elsewhere, Settings → Atlas → Audio turns it on for the whole house.

On a wall screen and a phone. The buttons are sized for a finger, and a player's controls wrap under its name on a phone. The volume slider shows the level as you drag and never jumps back under your finger. Sound, Share and long source lists open in their own panel. Anyone who can switch the lights can use them, wall screens included. Each press is one Home Assistant call, made with that person's own login.

Worth it if you have a whole-house audio system in Home Assistant (Control4, Crestron, Savant, Russound, Nuvo, Monoprice, Xantech, Triad, Denon, Yamaha or Anthem), speakers in several rooms, or a receiver or soundbar you'd like to set from the map.

Needs. The players in Home Assistant, from one of those systems or, with the tick above, any player with a volume. Rooms on the map that are Home Assistant areas with the same name. An administrator for the matching, the source names and the limits. Each part shows only when the player can do it: a player that can't change its source has no source buttons.

Free without it. The rest of a room's card: its lights and fans with All lights on and All lights off, its scenes and scripts one tap each, its locks, and Shades: every blind and shade in the room, how far open, with Open, Stop and Close for all at once (the Shades line, with the doors and windows in the ⋯ list, or Settings → Atlas → Shades). A room's thermostats are Climate & air quality's.

Limit. Without Pro, Audio is on or off for the whole house. Choosing it screen by screen needs Pro's new layout.Without Pro, Audio is on or off for the whole house, apart from wall screens, which each tick it for themselves. Choosing it screen by screen anywhere else needs Pro's new layout. A Control4 OS 2 controller doesn't list a room's sources, so its rooms have no source buttons.

Alarm & security

Arm the alarm from the hallway screen on your way out, and check from bed that the garage door is shut.

What you get

  • Your house alarm in the emergency button, top right of the Atlas. Its centre shows the alarm's shield in the colour of its state. Press it for the alarm's card: its state, what set it off when the alarm says (Alarmo does), and Arm home, Arm away, Arm night, Arm vacation or Arm custom bypass, whichever your alarm supports, or Disarm. Each one asks first, with a box for the alarm's code when it has one. With several alarms, the shield shows a triggered one first, then pending, armed and disarmed.
  • The door, garage and gate card. Tap a linked door, window or gate on the Atlas to see Closed & Locked, Closed, Unlocked or Open, with Open and Close for its opener (Trigger for a relay that works like the wall button), and Lock and Unlock for its lock.
  • Devices → Door Openers: mark a cover, switch, button or script as an opener, then link it and a lock to the door in Mapping → Atlas builder.
  • Openers and locks from plain relays: a momentary opener for a garage door or gate, a two-direction opener for a motorised window, blind or awning, and an electric door strike as a lock. PadSpan builds the Home Assistant script, cover or lock for you.
  • The emergency lights test. Test emergency lighting turns on every emergency light at once. Lights that were already on stay on when the test ends, Force off turns them all off, and each light has its own switch in the card. PadSpan finds the lights from Home Assistant groups with "emergency" in their name or ID, or from WLED lights with it in their name.

Where. The Atlas page for the button, its card and the door cards; Devices → Door Openers; and Mapping → Atlas builder for linking.

On a wall screen and a phone. The alarm works on a wall screen as it does anywhere, and nothing on its card leads off the page. On a phone it's the same button and card, on the Atlas page in the Home Assistant app.

Worth it if you have an alarm panel in Home Assistant (Alarmo, or your alarm system's integration), garage doors or gates, locks on outside doors, or emergency lighting.

Needs. A Home Assistant alarm panel for arming. Door and window sensors linked to their doors in Mapping, for the door card. A cover, switch, button or script for each opener, and a lock in Home Assistant for each lock.

Free without it. An alarm going off still shows. Its shield lights the emergency button, with nothing to press, and a row appears in the red banner on every PadSpan screen. Leak, smoke, gas and CO alarms turn their rooms red and send a notification. Locks lock and unlock on the map and in a room's card. Alert pulse (off until you turn it on) rings a room amber when a door or window has been open more than 6 hours, or a window is open in the rain. Shades are free too, with the doors and windows: every blind and shade in a room's card, with Open, Stop and Close. Garage doors, gates, doors and any cover linked as a door's opener are never shown under Shades; they stay this add-on's. An emergency lights test already running can always be ended.

Limit. Arming and disarming use the Home Assistant login of whoever is at the screen, so anyone who can use the alarm in Home Assistant can arm it from a shared wall screen.

Climate & air quality

A glance at the wall shows which bedroom is cold and which office has gone stuffy, and the bedroom's thermostat is in its card.

What you get

  • A ♨ Heatmap chip on the Atlas's layer bar. Each tap steps through Temperature, Humidity and CO₂, then off, offering only what your house has sensors for.
  • Rooms coloured from their own sensors. Temperature runs blue below 19 °C, green from 20.5 to 23.5 °C, amber at 25 and red at 28. Humidity is green from 40 to 60 %, orange when it's dry and blue when it's damp. CO₂ is green at 400 ppm, yellow at 1,000 and red at 1,500 and over.
  • Nothing crosses a wall. A sensor out on the deck never colours the living room, outdoor rooms stay clear, and a room with no sensor stays uncoloured. Readings more than an hour old are left out, and Fahrenheit sensors are converted.
  • Thermostats in a room's card: each thermostat in the room, with its temperature, its mode, and its setpoint with − and +. The room's temperature and humidity line at the top of the card comes with them.

Where. The Atlas page and Mapping → Atlas builder. Turn the heatmap on in Settings → Atlas → Heatmap chip on the Atlas (temperature, humidity, CO₂). For the thermostats, on a wall screen and with PadSpan Pro's new layout, each screen ticks Thermostats in its ⋯ list, in the group "Motion and sensors"; elsewhere, Settings → Atlas → Thermostats turns them on for the whole house. Both are off until you turn them on.

On a wall screen and a phone. The chip and the thermostats are on any screen that shows the Atlas. Anyone who can switch the lights can set a thermostat, each press one Home Assistant call made with their own login.

Worth it if you have a temperature, humidity or CO₂ sensor in most rooms, or a thermostat in several rooms.

Needs. Those sensors and thermostats in the Home Assistant areas that match your map's rooms.

Free without it. The temperature, humidity and air chips under room names, and Air in a room's card, stay free. Your sensors still show on the Atlas as markers, and in Home Assistant.

Limit. Without Pro, sensors aren't placed on the map, so each room takes one colour from the average of its own sensors. With Pro, placed sensors blend across a room.Placed sensors blend across a room. A room whose sensors aren't placed takes one colour from the average of its own sensors.

Vacation Mode & presets

On your way out for a week, turn on Vacation Mode on the Atlas, and the lights keep the house's usual evenings until you're back.

What you get

  • 🌴 Vacation Mode, first in the Whole house list on the Atlas. It learns each light's routine for each weekday from Home Assistant's history, then keeps switching the lights to match, with some randomness, until you turn it off.
  • A banner in the middle of the Atlas while it runs, with an Energy saving slider (lower means fewer lights on) and Disable, which asks first so a passing tap on a wall screen can't end it.
  • Options: the routine from last week (each day plays like the same day last week) or from the monthly average; Only after dark, starting when you choose between 30 minutes before sunset and 2 hours after; an end date; Pause while someone is home; Bedtime, when its lights go off until 5 am, moving up to 20 minutes either way each night; Longest on, from 1 to 6 hours; Most lights on at once, from 1 to 12; up to 10 Evening lights, such as a porch or hall light; and Preview, which shows the next 24 hours and switches nothing.
  • Whole house presets. Set remembers how every light and fan is set right now (on or off, brightness, colour, effect and speed) under a name you type. Apply puts the whole house back that way, turning off what was off, after a second tap on "Yes, change the whole house". Locks are never included.
  • For your own automations, binary_sensor.padspan_vacation_mode and the padspan_ha_vacation_mode event. When someone turns Vacation Mode off, you get a notice saying who, when, and from which app or browser.

Where. The Atlas page and Mapping → Atlas builder. With Pro's new layout, the list sits in the ★ presets drawer.

On a wall screen and a phone. While Vacation Mode runs, its banner sits in the middle of a wall screen. On a phone, the Whole house list works the same on the Atlas page in the Home Assistant app.

Worth it if you have trips away, or a few whole-house settings you use often, like movie night or everything off at bedtime.

Needs. Home Assistant's history for your lights (it keeps 10 days unless you changed it); the last-week routine needs the 7 days before you leave. An administrator turns Vacation Mode on and off. It only ever switches lights and fans.

Free without it. Your Home Assistant scenes and scripts in each room's card, one tap each, and All lights on and All lights off for each room.

Limit. The routine comes from your lights' history, so a new install has nothing to learn from yet. It says so: "Nothing yet: there is no light history to learn a routine from."

Advanced smart lighting

Colour bulbs behind a wall dimmer come back in the right colour, follow the day, dim for sleep and wake you slowly in the morning.

What you get

  • Follow the day: cool and bright at midday, warm at sunrise and sunset, dim and warmer through the night. Sleep is one dim, warm setting, and there's a bedtime and a night light.
  • A wake-up: on the days and at the time you choose, a team that is off comes on at its dimmest and warmest and fades to full light by then.
  • WLED's Advanced controls in a WLED light's card: layout, effects, presets and playlists, LEDs, sync and team, and Exact look, which makes a strip come on the same way every time. A WLED light's basic controls stay free.
  • On the Atlas, the lighting looks:
    • Automorph: a soft glow from each light into its room.
    • Fit room: never draws a light bigger than its room.
    • Light spread: lines of equal light on the floor.
    • Scene: a colour wash across the floor, previewed on the lit lights and sent to them with Apply.
    • Ripple: a brightness pulse spreading from where you tap.
    • Hide untouched: draws only the lights you set up.
    They draw on Showcase's Classic theme, which comes with this add-on.
  • Fixture shapes, with their size and angle, and the type override: give a light its real shape and size, turned to its real angle, like a pendant over the table or a strip under the kitchen cabinets, and say what kind of light it is.

Where. Settings → Atlas → Power teams, a WLED light's card, and Mapping → Atlas builder and the Atlas toolbar for the looks and shapes.

On a wall screen and a phone. A power team is one light on any screen: a tap switches it, a drag dims it and a hold opens its card. The looks draw on any screen that shows the Atlas.

Worth it if you have smart colour bulbs on a wall dimmer or switch (Zigbee bulbs on a Control4 dimmer, say), WLED strips, or a wall screen where the lights should look like light.

Needs. The lights in Home Assistant, and WLED lights for the WLED part. An administrator makes and changes teams, the wake-up and the day's curve.

Free without it. A team already made keeps working as one light: on, off, dimming, its power and each bulb. It just doesn't follow the day, sleep or wake you. A Bright Pro key keeps everything it had.

Wi-Fi coverage

When a smart plug keeps dropping off the Wi-Fi, see which access point it's stuck on and which room is weak.

What you get

  • A Wi-Fi coverage tab with a map of each floor, drawn on your own rooms and coloured from red at −85 dBm to green at −45 dBm.
  • 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz as separate maps, since most smart devices use 2.4 GHz and most phones use 5 GHz. Show all access points, or one at a time to see its reach.
  • A room table with each room's median signal, a word for it from excellent to very weak, and how long it was measured.
  • What to fix: UniFi Utopia's findings, most urgent first, each with what to do. Click one to see it on the plan. They include devices that drop off, devices on a far access point while a nearer one hears them better, and rooms where phones sit on weak Wi-Fi.
  • Where the access points are, placed in Mapping or estimated from the devices that hear each one best, and which Wi-Fi device is which phone when their names differ. Both are for administrators, and placing an access point in Mapping needs Pro.
  • With a robot vacuum doing PadSpan's room location runs, its readings at floor level as a layer of their own, and with the Robot vacuum add-on, its Wi-Fi while it cleans.

Where. A tab in PadSpan's panel, under More tools unless you move it. It appears only while UniFi Utopia's survey answers.

On a wall screen and a phone. It's a tab for a desk or a phone; wall screens don't show it.

Worth it if you have UniFi access points and smart devices that drop off the Wi-Fi.

Needs. UniFi Utopia, another integration of mine, version 0.4.3 or later, on the same Home Assistant, with a UniFi controller and UniFi access points. Its Wi-Fi survey makes the map, and your Wi-Fi coverage key unlocks it. The readings come from phones that sit still for about a minute where PadSpan has them, and from PadSpan's receivers, so the map fills in over days. With Pro, lights and devices placed on the map add readings too.

Free without it. UniFi Utopia's own features work the same with or without it.

Limit. Devices differ in their transmitters and antennas, so a difference between two neighbouring devices can come from the devices themselves.

Presence insights

When the keys go missing, Locate walks you to their tag room by room. Busy Times shows which rooms the house really uses, and when. Forensics tells you whether an unknown tracker was near the house last night.

What you get

  • 📍 Locate in Follow. Pick your own phone or tag under You are carrying, open the tag you're looking for, and Locate lists the rooms to walk through, with the next one highlighted and any floor to go up or down. In the same room it switches to a live distance in metres, getting closer or further.
  • Busy Times in Traceback: which rooms are busiest over the last 1, 3 or 7 days, across everything PadSpan tracks. One box per room, busiest first, each with a 24-hour bar chart and its busiest hour marked.

And Forensics, which shows which Bluetooth devices were near a receiver in any time window:

  • Recording. Switch on Enable presence-session recording in Settings → Features → Forensics. PadSpan then records every Bluetooth device near your receivers as sessions (start, end, strongest signal and which receivers heard it), sampled every 60 seconds. A session ends after 5 minutes without a signal.
  • The Forensics tab, once recording is on. Pick a window (the last hour, 24 hours or 7 days, or your own) to see the devices recorded in it, sorted by time, then lower-confidence possible matches. Hide your own labelled devices, and export the results as CSV.
  • How long it keeps them: 7, 14 (the default), 30, 60 or 90 days.

Not Traceback's Insights. Traceback also has a tab called Insights, which is free: time in each room, entries and peak occupancy, with CSV export. Presence insights is this add-on, with Locate, Busy Times and Forensics.

Where. Follow → 📍 Locate, Traceback → Busy Times, and the Forensics tab, with its recording switch in Settings → Features → Forensics.

On a wall screen and a phone. Locate is for the phone in your hand. It needs no camera or compass, so it works on a wall tablet too. Busy Times reads best on a bigger screen, and Forensics is a desk tool.

Worth it if you have Bluetooth tags on things that go missing, like keys or a bag, you tune heating and automations to how rooms are used, or a device keeps turning up that you don't recognise.

Needs. Bluetooth receivers and tracked phones or tags. Locate needs rooms drawn on your floor plan, since it walks from room to room. Busy Times uses Traceback's 7 days of history and works without a floor plan. Forensics records live data only, with PadSpan showing your own house (Live) rather than the sample house.

Privacy. Forensics records neighbours', visitors' and passers-by's devices too. The recordings stay on your Home Assistant and are never uploaded. Laws on recording wireless identifiers vary, and using it lawfully is up to you.

Free without it. Traceback's Insights tab and the 📍 flash that shows a device on the map, PadSpan's presence tracking, Traceback and each device's history. If the add-on ends, Forensics stops recording, and what was recorded stays searchable and exportable until its time is up.

Limit. Locate gives directions room by room rather than an arrow, and you pick which device is yours, since a shared screen can't tell who is looking at it. Forensics calls its results "investigative leads, not proof". A Bluetooth address isn't a person. Phones change their addresses every 15 minutes to 24 hours, addresses can be faked, and a missing record doesn't mean a missing device. It mostly finds devices with a fixed address, such as earbuds, trackers and gadgets.

If you bought Forensics. Forensics was sold on its own before, and isn't any more. A key that holds Forensics has all of Presence insights.

Questions

Do I need an add-on to use PadSpan?

No. The free version needs no key and doesn't run out. An add-on adds one area to it, and Pro includes every add-on.

Add-ons or Pro?

All the add-ons together cost less than two years of Pro. Pro also has what no add-on sells: every light and device placed where it is, Live Aboard and Furnish, wall mode,Furnish, people figures and the editor in the 3D house, the wall screen's night dimming and other extras, the Atlas as your Overview, and people and tags on the map. Pro also covers up to 5 homes (with more than 2, each keeps its usage report on), and every new PadSpan feature is included while its key runs. The prices for both are side by side on the home page, and you can try all of Pro free for 90 days.

Does an add-on give me wall mode or the 3D house?

No, both are part of Pro. Wall mode fits the whole house to the screen, keeps the screen awake and can dim it at night. On an install with add-ons, the Atlas page still runs on a wall tablet with the classic layout, and each add-on's parts on the Atlas work there.

You don't need one: the plain wall screen and the 3D house are free, with no key. The wall fits the whole house to the screen, sized for the room, and each add-on's parts on the Atlas work there. Pro adds the wall's night dimming, burn-in shift, floors in turn and Fully Kiosk motion wake, and Furnish, people figures and the editor in the 3D house.

Which PadSpan version do I need?

PadSpan 0.38.300 or later for the add-ons. This page describes the latest PadSpan, so update to get everything on it.

Is anything I set up lost when an add-on ends?

No. Your settings stay stored for when a key comes back, and Forensics keeps what it recorded until its time is up.

Who in the house can use them?

Anyone whose Home Assistant login can use the device. Audio, the thermostats and the alarm run with each person's own login, on wall screens too. Matching players to rooms, turning Vacation Mode on or off and entering the key take an administrator.
