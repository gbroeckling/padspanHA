Add-ons: one feature each, no subscription

Each add-on adds one thing to the free PadSpan, such as the music in each room on the map, or arming your alarm from the map. One payment covers 2 years on up to 3 homes, and one key carries every add-on you buy. Add-ons need PadSpan 0.38.300 or later. PadSpan Pro includes every one of them.

PadSpan Pro includes all nine add-ons, plus what no add-on sells:

  • every light and device placed exactly where it is, in its real shape
  • Live Aboard, the 3D house you look into like a house in The Sims, and Furnish to put furniture in it
  • wall mode, with the Atlas's new layout
  • the Atlas as your Overview
  • people and tags on the map

You can try all of Pro free for 90 days, with no card.

Most add-ons add to the Atlas, PadSpan's house map in the Home Assistant sidebar, where you tap a light to switch it. Without Pro, the Atlas shows each light in its room rather than exactly where it hangs, and the add-ons work on that map.

Control the house from the map

Three add-ons that put more of the house a tap away on the Atlas.

Audio

Turn the kitchen music down from the hallway screen, and press Room off as you leave a room.

What you get

  • A speaker beside a room's name on the Atlas while one of its players is on, struck through when it's muted. Tap it, or the room's name, to open the room's card.
  • Each player in the room's card, with Mute, −, its level from 0 to 100, +, and ⏻ to turn it off. Only the buttons the player supports appear. A player that steps its own volume, as Control4's do, shows the level it reports.
  • Shades: every blind and shade in the room, how far open they are, and Open, Stop and Close for all of them at once. Garage doors, gates and doors are left out.
  • Climate: each thermostat in the room, with its temperature, its mode, and the setpoint with − and +.
  • Room off, first in the card, turns off the room's lights and players that are on in one press.
  • The room's temperature and humidity at the top of the card.
  • Match rooms: an administrator picks the room each player is in, once. PadSpan guesses first from the player's Home Assistant area or its name. Several players can share a room, a Control4 zone and a Denon zone, say.
  • Include Home Assistant speakers, on the matching card, adds Music Assistant, ESPHome, Cast and TV players. It's off unless you tick it.

Where. The Atlas page. Turn it on for the whole house in Settings → Atlas → Audio. With PadSpan Pro's new layout, each screen ticks Audio in its ⋯ list instead.

On a wall screen and a phone. The buttons are sized for a finger, and a player's controls stay on one line, wrapping under its name on a phone. Anyone who can switch the lights can use them, wall screens included. Each press is one Home Assistant call, made with that person's own login.

Worth it if you have a whole-house audio system in Home Assistant (Control4, Crestron, Savant, Russound, Nuvo, Monoprice, Xantech, Triad, Denon, Yamaha or Anthem), speakers in several rooms, or motorised shades and a thermostat in each room.

Needs. The players in Home Assistant, from one of those systems or, with the tick above, any player with a volume. Rooms on the map that are Home Assistant areas with the same name. Shades and thermostats in the room's area. An administrator for the matching.

Free without it. The rest of a room's card: its lights and fans with All lights on and All lights off, its scenes and scripts one tap each, and its locks.

Limit. Without Pro, Audio is on or off for the whole house. Choosing it screen by screen needs Pro's new layout.

Alarm & security

Arm the alarm from the hallway screen on your way out, and check from bed that the garage door is shut.

What you get

  • Your house alarm in the emergency button, top right of the Atlas. Its centre shows the alarm's shield in the colour of its state. Press it for the alarm's card: its state, what set it off when the alarm says (Alarmo does), and Arm home, Arm away, Arm night, Arm vacation or Arm custom bypass, whichever your alarm supports, or Disarm. Each one asks first, with a box for the alarm's code when it has one. With several alarms, the shield shows a triggered one first, then pending, armed and disarmed.
  • The emergency lights test. Test emergency lighting turns on every emergency light at once. Lights that were already on stay on when the test ends, Force off turns them all off, and each light has its own switch in the card. PadSpan finds the lights from Home Assistant groups with "emergency" in their name or ID, or from WLED lights with it in their name.
  • The door, garage and gate card. Tap a linked door, window or gate on the Atlas to see Closed & Locked, Closed, Unlocked or Open, with Open and Close for its opener (Trigger for a relay that works like the wall button), and Lock and Unlock for its lock.
  • Devices → Door Openers: mark a cover, switch, button or script as an opener, then link it and a lock to the door in Mapping → Atlas builder.
  • Openers and locks from plain relays: a momentary opener for a garage door or gate, a two-direction opener for a motorised window, blind or awning, and an electric door strike as a lock. PadSpan builds the Home Assistant cover or lock for you.

Where. The Atlas page for the button, its card and the door cards; Devices → Door Openers; and Mapping → Atlas builder for linking.

On a wall screen and a phone. The alarm works on a wall screen as it does anywhere, and nothing on its card leads off the page. On a phone it's the same button and card, on the Atlas page in the Home Assistant app.

Worth it if you have an alarm panel in Home Assistant (Alarmo, or your alarm system's integration), garage doors or gates, locks on outside doors, or emergency lighting.

Needs. A Home Assistant alarm panel for arming. Door and window sensors linked to their doors in Mapping, for the door card. A cover, switch, button or script for each opener, and a lock in Home Assistant for each lock.

Free without it. An alarm going off still shows. Its shield lights the emergency button, with nothing to press, and a row appears in the red banner on every PadSpan screen. Leak, smoke, gas and CO alarms turn their rooms red and send a notification. Locks lock and unlock on the map and in a room's card. Alert pulse (off until you turn it on) rings a room amber when a door or window has been open more than 6 hours, or a window is open in the rain. The emergency lights test was free until PadSpan 0.38.300 and is part of this add-on now; a test already running can always be ended.

Limit. Arming and disarming use the Home Assistant login of whoever is at the screen, so anyone who can use the alarm in Home Assistant can arm it from a shared wall screen.

Vacation Mode & presets

On your way out for a week, turn on Vacation Mode on the Atlas, and the lights keep the house's usual evenings until you're back.

What you get

  • 🌴 Vacation Mode, first in the Whole house list on the Atlas. It learns each light's routine for each weekday from Home Assistant's history, then keeps switching the lights to match, with some randomness, until you turn it off.
  • A banner in the middle of the Atlas while it runs, with an Energy saving slider (lower means fewer lights on) and Disable, which asks first so a passing tap on a wall screen can't end it.
  • Options: the routine from last week (each day plays like the same day last week) or from the monthly average; Only after dark, starting when you choose between 30 minutes before sunset and 2 hours after; an end date; Pause while someone is home; Bedtime, when its lights go off until 5 am, moving up to 20 minutes either way each night; Longest on, from 1 to 6 hours; Most lights on at once, from 1 to 12; up to 10 Evening lights, such as a porch or hall light; and Preview, which shows the next 24 hours and switches nothing.
  • Whole house presets. Set remembers how every light and fan is set right now (on or off, brightness, colour, effect and speed) under a name you type. Apply puts the whole house back that way, turning off what was off, after a second tap on "Yes, change the whole house". Locks are never included.
  • For your own automations, binary_sensor.padspan_vacation_mode and the padspan_ha_vacation_mode event. When someone turns Vacation Mode off, you get a notice saying who, when, and from which app or browser.

Where. The Atlas page and Mapping → Atlas builder. With Pro's new layout, the list sits in the ★ presets drawer.

On a wall screen and a phone. While Vacation Mode runs, its banner sits in the middle of a wall screen. On a phone, the Whole house list works the same on the Atlas page in the Home Assistant app.

Worth it if you have trips away, or a few whole-house settings you use often, like movie night or everything off at bedtime.

Needs. Home Assistant's history for your lights (it keeps 10 days unless you changed it); the last-week routine needs the 7 days before you leave. An administrator turns Vacation Mode on and off. It only ever switches lights and fans.

Free without it. Your Home Assistant scenes and scripts in each room's card, one tap each, and All lights on and All lights off for each room.

Limit. The routine comes from your lights' history, so a new install has nothing to learn from yet. It says so: "Nothing yet: there is no light history to learn a routine from."

See the house on the map

Three add-ons that change what the map shows you, and how it looks.

Climate map

A glance at the wall shows which bedroom is cold and which office has gone stuffy.

What you get

  • A ♨ Heatmap chip on the Atlas's layer bar. Each tap steps through Temperature, Humidity and CO₂, then off, offering only what your house has sensors for.
  • Rooms coloured from their own sensors. Temperature runs blue below 19 °C, green from 20.5 to 23.5 °C, amber at 25 and red at 28. Humidity is green from 40 to 60 %, orange when it's dry and blue when it's damp. CO₂ is green at 400 ppm, yellow at 1,000 and red at 1,500 and over.
  • Nothing crosses a wall. A sensor out on the deck never colours the living room, outdoor rooms stay clear, and a room with no sensor stays uncoloured. Readings more than an hour old are left out, and Fahrenheit sensors are converted.

Where. The Atlas page and Mapping → Atlas builder. Turn it on in Settings → Atlas → Heatmap chip on the Atlas (temperature, humidity, CO₂).

On a wall screen and a phone. It's a chip on the Atlas, so it's on any screen that shows the Atlas.

Worth it if you have a temperature, humidity or CO₂ sensor in most rooms.

Needs. Those sensors in the Home Assistant areas that match your map's rooms.

Free without it. Your sensors still show on the Atlas as markers, and in Home Assistant. Setting a thermostat from a room's card is part of Audio, not this add-on.

Limit. Without Pro, sensors aren't placed on the map, so each room takes one colour from the average of its own sensors. With Pro, placed sensors blend across a room.

Wi-Fi coverage

When a smart plug keeps dropping off the Wi-Fi, see which access point it's stuck on and which room is weak.

What you get

  • A Wi-Fi coverage tab with a map of each floor, drawn on your own rooms and coloured from red at −85 dBm to green at −45 dBm.
  • 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz as separate maps, since most smart devices use 2.4 GHz and most phones use 5 GHz. Show all access points, or one at a time to see its reach.
  • A room table with each room's median signal, a word for it from excellent to very weak, and how long it was measured.
  • What to fix: UniFi Utopia's findings, most urgent first, each with what to do. Click one to see it on the plan. They include devices that drop off, devices on a far access point while a nearer one hears them better, and rooms where phones sit on weak Wi-Fi.
  • Where the access points are, placed in Mapping or estimated from the devices that hear each one best, and which Wi-Fi device is which phone when their names differ. Both are for administrators, and placing an access point in Mapping needs Pro.
  • With a robot vacuum doing PadSpan's room location runs, its readings at floor level as a layer of their own.

Where. A tab in PadSpan's panel, under More tools unless you move it. It appears only while UniFi Utopia's survey answers.

On a wall screen and a phone. It's a tab for a desk or a phone; wall screens don't show it.

Worth it if you have UniFi access points and smart devices that drop off the Wi-Fi.

Needs. UniFi Utopia, another integration of mine, on the same Home Assistant, with a UniFi controller and UniFi access points. Its Wi-Fi survey is still a test build. The readings come from phones that sit still for about a minute where PadSpan has them, and from PadSpan's receivers, so the map fills in over days. With Pro, lights and devices placed on the map add readings too.

Free without it. UniFi Utopia's own features work the same with or without it.

Limit. Devices differ in their transmitters and antennas, so a difference between two neighbouring devices can come from the devices themselves.

Looks pack

Give the wall screen a look that suits its room, and the panel a light look by day.

What you get

  • Four panel themes in Settings → Appearance → Theme: Graphite, Ocean and Dusk, which are Night in charcoal, navy or plum, and Day, the panel redrawn in light colours. With Follow the sun on, the panel uses Day while the sun is up.
  • Showcase on the Atlas: 20 themes for the whole map, from Classic and Neo HUD to Hygge Warmth and Obsidian Noir. See all twenty.
  • The West Coast Forest, the Showcase theme with trees drawn round the house.
  • Look presets: save a whole look under a name (the theme and Showcase's other settings, and if you like the floor, spacing and layout), and Apply brings it back.

Where. The themes change PadSpan's main panel in every browser. The Atlas page keeps its own dark look with every theme; Showcase and its presets change that, from the Atlas toolbar under Presentation.

On a wall screen and a phone. Showcase is the look for a wall screen. The panel themes are for phones and desks, where you use PadSpan's main panel.

Worth it if you have a wall screen that's part of how a room looks, or you'd like a light panel by day.

Needs. Nothing beyond PadSpan.

Free without it. Night, Light (every colour inverted, for anyone who can't use a dark screen) and High contrast.

Limit. Without Pro, lights aren't placed, so Showcase draws them together in their rooms. Automorph and Fit room come with Pro.

Presence and history

Three add-ons for houses with Bluetooth receivers, about what happened and where things are.

Full house activity

Listed as Time travel in PadSpan 0.38.300 and 0.38.301.

Back from a day out, play the house back and see which lights stayed on all afternoon and when the back door opened.

What you get

  • The 🏠 Full house activity switch in Traceback → Playback. Turn it on and the floor stack makes way for the Atlas map, and the house plays back with the phones and tags PadSpan tracks on top. Traceback's own play, pause and scrub controls drive it.
  • 💡 Devices: every device the Atlas shows plays back through its states for the time you pick, including lights, doors, locks, motion, temperature, humidity and air. What changed is ringed on the map.
  • Any range Traceback offers, from 5 minutes to 7 days, or a window you set.
  • Lights that Vacation Mode switched are marked as Vacation Mode's.

Where. The Traceback tab in PadSpan's panel.

On a wall screen and a phone. It's for sitting down with, at a desk or on a tablet or phone. It isn't part of the Atlas page a wall screen shows.

Worth it if you have Bluetooth receivers and a floor plan, and lights, doors and sensors you'd like to look back on.

Needs. A floor plan, since Traceback replays on one. Bluetooth receivers for phones and tags; Traceback keeps them for 7 days. Home Assistant's recorder for devices. PadSpan records nothing extra for them, so devices go back as far as the recorder keeps, and anything left out of the recorder won't play back.

Free without it. Traceback's own Playback of phones and tags on the floor stack, New Objects, Insights, and Share a replay, which makes a 10 to 30 second clip. These need a Bluetooth receiver too.

Limit. The add-on draws the map your install has. Without Pro, lights sit together in their rooms rather than where they hang, and temperature, humidity and air readings play back only for sensors placed on the map.

Presence insights

When the keys go missing, Locate walks you to their tag room by room. Busy Times shows which rooms the house really uses, and when.

What you get

  • 📍 Locate in Follow. Pick your own phone or tag under You are carrying, open the tag you're looking for, and Locate lists the rooms to walk through, with the next one highlighted and any floor to go up or down. In the same room it switches to a live distance in metres, getting closer or further.
  • Busy Times in Traceback: which rooms are busiest over the last 1, 3 or 7 days, across everything PadSpan tracks. One box per room, busiest first, each with a 24-hour bar chart and its busiest hour marked.

Where. Follow → 📍 Locate, and Traceback → Busy Times.

On a wall screen and a phone. Locate is for the phone in your hand. It needs no camera or compass, so it works on a wall tablet too. Busy Times reads best on a bigger screen.

Worth it if you have Bluetooth tags on things that go missing, like keys or a bag, or you tune heating and automations to how rooms are used.

Needs. Bluetooth receivers and tracked phones or tags. Locate needs rooms drawn on your floor plan, since it walks from room to room. Busy Times uses Traceback's 7 days of history and works without a floor plan.

Free without it. Traceback's Insights (time in each room, entries and peak occupancy, with CSV export) and the 📍 flash that shows a device on the map.

Limit. Locate gives directions room by room rather than an arrow, and you pick which device is yours, since a shared screen can't tell who is looking at it.

Forensics

Check whether an unknown tracker or a pair of earbuds was near the house last night, and for how long.

What you get

  • Recording. Switch on Enable presence-session recording in Settings → Features → Forensics. PadSpan then records every Bluetooth device near your scanners as sessions (start, end, strongest signal and which scanners heard it), sampled every 60 seconds. A session ends after 5 minutes without a signal.
  • The Forensics tab, once recording is on. Pick a window (the last hour, 24 hours or 7 days, or your own) to see the devices recorded in it, sorted by time, then lower-confidence possible matches. Hide your own labelled devices, and export the results as CSV.
  • How long it keeps them: 7, 14 (the default), 30, 60 or 90 days.

Where. Settings → Features, and the Forensics tab.

On a wall screen and a phone. It's a desk tool.

Worth it if you have several Bluetooth scanners and a question about a device that keeps turning up, such as a tracker you don't recognise.

Needs. Bluetooth receivers, with PadSpan showing your own house (Live) rather than the sample house. It records live data only.

Privacy. It records neighbours', visitors' and passers-by's devices too. The recordings stay on your Home Assistant and are never uploaded. Laws on recording wireless identifiers vary, and using it lawfully is up to you.

Free without it. PadSpan's presence tracking, Traceback and each device's history. If the add-on ends, recording stops, and what was recorded stays searchable and exportable until its time is up.

Limit. The panel calls the results "investigative leads, not proof". A Bluetooth address isn't a person. Phones change their addresses every 15 minutes to 24 hours, addresses can be faked, and a missing record doesn't mean a missing device. It mostly finds devices with a fixed address, such as earbuds, trackers and gadgets.

Terms for every add-on

  • 2 years for one payment. There's no subscription.
  • Up to 3 homes, meaning three Home Assistants. A reinstalled Home Assistant takes the place of a home that hasn't checked in for 14 days, and a home silent for 45 days frees its place by itself.
  • One key for all of them. Each add-on on it has its own end date. Buy another with the same email and it joins that key.
  • Entering the key. In PadSpan's panel, open Settings → Features → PadSpan licence and press Enter licence key. It takes a Home Assistant administrator, with PadSpan in Advanced mode, since Basic mode's Settings shows only Appearance. The card then lists each add-on with its date.
  • PadSpan 0.38.300 or later. An add-on key works only there.
  • Offline. The end dates are kept on your Home Assistant, so add-ons keep working when the internet or the licence server is down.
  • 14 days' grace. When an add-on's 2 years end, it keeps working for 14 more days, and the licence card says so. After that it's locked until you renew, and nothing you set up is lost.
  • The Pro trial. With only an add-on key, you can still start the 90-day trial of all of Pro. Your add-on key is kept aside and comes back by itself when the trial and its 14 days' grace end. If you enter a different key during the trial, PadSpan uses that one and stops holding the add-on key aside. The trial needs the usage report on, and it stays on until the trial ends.
  • Where you meet them. A locked feature says what it needs, for example "PadSpan Pro or the Looks pack add-on". Some parts aren't there at all until the key is in (Audio, the heatmap chip, the Wi-Fi coverage tab, the Full house activity switch and the Whole house list), so the licence card lists every add-on you don't have yet, with a link to its price.

Questions

Do I need an add-on to use PadSpan?

No. The free version needs no key and doesn't run out. An add-on adds one feature to it, and Pro includes every add-on.

Add-ons or Pro?

All nine add-ons together cost less than two years of Pro. Pro also has what no add-on sells: every light and device placed where it is, Live Aboard and Furnish, wall mode, the Atlas as your Overview, and people and tags on the map. Pro also covers up to 5 homes (with more than 2, each keeps its usage report on), and every new PadSpan feature is included while its key runs. The prices for both are side by side on the home page, and you can try all of Pro free for 90 days.

Does an add-on give me wall mode or the 3D house?

No, both are part of Pro. Wall mode fits the whole house to the screen, keeps the screen awake and can dim it at night. On an install with add-ons, the Atlas page still runs on a wall tablet with the classic layout, and each add-on's parts on the Atlas work there.

Which PadSpan version do I need?

0.38.300 or later, which is a beta release for now.

Is anything I set up lost when an add-on ends?

No. Your settings stay stored for when a key comes back, and Forensics keeps what it recorded until its time is up.

Who in the house can use them?

Anyone whose Home Assistant login can use the device. Audio and the alarm run with each person's own login, on wall screens too. Matching players to rooms, turning Vacation Mode on or off and entering the key take an administrator.
