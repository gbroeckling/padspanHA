Bermuda, ESPresense or PadSpan?

Three ways to get Bluetooth room presence in Home Assistant. All three answer the same question, which room each phone, watch and tag is in, in different ways. This is what each one does, from their own docs as of October 2026, and which fits which house. PadSpan's developer wrote it, so the sources are at the end.

The short version

  • Bermuda if you want room sensors from the ESPHome Bluetooth proxies you already have, with very little to set up. It has no map.
  • ESPresense if you're happy to flash its own firmware onto ESP32 nodes. Each node does more on its own, and the ESPresense Companion app adds a floor-plan view, positions and a 3D view.
  • PadSpan if you want a map of your house inside Home Assistant, on the same ESPHome proxies Bermuda uses, with a calibration walk and alerts built in. It also runs next to Bermuda, so you don't have to choose.

Bermuda

Bermuda turns the Bluetooth adverts your proxies already hear into Home Assistant sensors: which area each device is in, its distance from the nearest proxy (distances to each proxy can be switched on), and a device tracker. It works with ESPHome Bluetooth proxies, Shelly Gen2+ devices and, more basically, USB Bluetooth adapters. It recognises iPhones and Apple Watches through Home Assistant's Private BLE Device, and iBeacons even when their address changes. It smooths distances by averaging recent readings, ignores readings that would mean a device moved away faster than a set speed, and resists switching straight back to an area it just left. Its calibration is guided, with settings for the whole house, each proxy and each device. Its wiki says a map of your house "is not yet a thing" and that one is planned: today you get sensors for the areas your proxies are in, and a Floor sensor since version 0.8.5.

Good fit: you already have ESPHome Bluetooth proxies, and sensors for your automations are all you want.

ESPresense

ESPresense is its own firmware for ESP32 boards. Each node filters and identifies the devices it hears (counting them is in beta), can also host motion and environment sensors, and reports over MQTT to Home Assistant (and to Homebridge or Domoticz). You enroll an iPhone or Apple Watch on a node's web page to capture its key (Android phones don't give one out). The separate Companion app takes your rooms as corner coordinates in a YAML file (MagicPlan can draw the plan, and ESPresense's Floorplan Creator turns measurements into the YAML), works out X/Y/Z positions, picks the most likely floor, calibrates its nodes automatically (each node can also be tuned by hand), and shows the result on a floor-plan view and a multi-floor 3D view. It doesn't use ESPHome Bluetooth proxies: the nodes run ESPresense instead.

Good fit: you're happy to dedicate ESP32 boards to it and run the Companion app, and you want positions more than room names.

PadSpan

PadSpan reads Home Assistant's own Bluetooth data, so the ESPHome proxies and Shelly scanners Bermuda uses serve it too, and ESPresense nodes can feed it over MQTT once you switch that on. Rooms come from each scanner's Home Assistant area, the scanner that hears a device best winning, as in Bermuda, so with a scanner in each room it works without a floor plan; with one, you draw your rooms on it and see everyone on a map, one floor at a time or all floors stacked. With three or more scanners placed on a measured plan it can put a device between them, even in a room with no scanner of its own. A walk around the house with your phone teaches it what each room's signals look like, and it smooths readings and makes a new room win a vote before it moves anyone. It recognises iPhones and Apple Watches through Private BLE Device (the key can come from a Mac or irk-capture), with a setup guide; follows tags (AirTags and SmartTags only experimentally); can alert you when a device changes room; and keeps a movement history you can play back. It wants more than 2 GB of RAM on your Home Assistant machine (a 2 GB machine can turn on Low-memory mode). PadSpan is free and open source; an optional paid Pro key adds the 3D house (Live Aboard) and a few extra tools, and you can try all of Pro free for 90 days, no card (from 0.38.180, the trial needs the opt-in usage report on).

Good fit: you want to see your house, not just sensors, and keep the proxies you have.

Can I run them together?

PadSpan and Bermuda: yes. PadSpan installs next to Bermuda, uses the same proxies and leaves Bermuda as it is. By default, devices Bermuda tracks show up in PadSpan in the room Bermuda's Area sensor reports; Bermuda and PadSpan together explains how. PadSpan can also take its readings from ESPresense nodes over MQTT.

Room Assistant, the fourth name in older comparisons, hasn't had a release since March 2022.
