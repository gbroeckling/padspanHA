Bermuda and PadSpan together

Already running Bermuda? Keep it. PadSpan installs next to it, listens to the same ESPHome Bluetooth proxies, leaves Bermuda as it is, and shows every phone, watch and tag on a floor plan of your house, live.

Try the demo in your browser Open PadSpan in HACS

What each one does

Bermuda works out which area each Bluetooth device is in from how strongly your proxies hear it, and gives you Home Assistant sensors for it. It is quick to set up and a lot of people run it. It has no map.

PadSpan answers the same question its own way and shows you the answer: a floor plan of your house, one floor at a time or all floors stacked, with every person, phone, watch, tag and beacon in the room it is in. It learns your rooms from a calibration walk and smooths and votes on each reading, so a device doesn't flip between rooms every time a signal dips. It also gives Home Assistant room, distance and home/away sensors, and can email you when a device you follow changes room.

Running both on the same proxies

  • Same hardware, no reflashing. PadSpan reads Home Assistant's own Bluetooth data, the same data Bermuda uses, so your ESPHome Bluetooth proxies serve both at once. ESPresense nodes work too, over MQTT.
  • Bermuda stays as it is. Installing, reloading or removing PadSpan doesn't change Bermuda's devices, entities or settings, and a test in PadSpan's code fails if new code tries to. The only changes PadSpan ever makes to another integration are buttons you press yourself, one at a time. The README lists them under What PadSpan never touches.
  • Your Bermuda devices show up. Each device Bermuda tracks appears in PadSpan as one device, in the room Bermuda's Area sensor reports, already smoothed by Bermuda. If you'd rather PadSpan used only its own readings, tick Ignore Bermuda in Settings → Presence.
  • Phones you already set up keep working. iPhones and Apple Watches you added to Home Assistant's Private BLE Device are recognised by the same key (the IRK) and named as you named them, so there is nothing to capture twice. Android phones work through the Home Assistant Companion app's BLE Transmitter.

What your number of proxies buys

ProxiesWhat PadSpan can do
One in the houseHome or away, not rooms.
One in each room you want told apartIt picks the room by which proxy hears a device best, the way Bermuda does, so expect rooms about as good as Bermuda's. A calibration walk can correct some of those calls.
Three or more on a floor, placed on a measured floor planIt can also work out where a device is between the proxies, even in a room with no proxy of its own. This is where it can beat "nearest proxy".

You don't have to take that on trust. From version 0.38.166, PadSpan's Calibration screen shows how often it gets the room right on spots it was not calibrated on, in your own house. More in How many Bluetooth proxies do I need?

Setting it up next to Bermuda

  1. In HACS, search for PadSpan HA and download it (it's in the HACS default list). Restart Home Assistant, then add the integration under Settings → Devices & services.
  2. Open PadSpan HA from the sidebar. It starts on sample data so you can look around; switch it to Live (top right) when you're ready. Your proxies are found automatically.
  3. The setup wizard then takes you through five steps: upload a floor plan, set its scale, draw the rooms, place your proxies where they really are, and calibrate by walking the rooms with your phone.

Bermuda keeps running the whole time, and your automations stay on its sensors until you decide to change them.

Free and Pro

PadSpan is free and open source (GPL-3.0). Room presence, floor plans, the maps, calibration, the sensors and the alerts all cost nothing. PadSpan Pro is an optional key that adds the 3D house (Live Aboard) and extra tools such as Forensics, Busy Times, Locate and exact light placement. See Pro on the main page.

Worth knowing before you start

  • Memory. More than 2 GB of RAM is recommended. Near a busy street PadSpan hears thousands of passing devices; on a 2 GB machine, turn on Low-memory mode in Settings → Presence. The minimum requirements page has the measured numbers.
  • A room with one proxy can still get confused. More proxies and a calibration walk help most.
  • Every house is different. Walls, floors and where the proxies sit change how well any Bluetooth system does. The Calibration screen tells you how PadSpan does in yours, and if it does badly, please open an issue.
