What can Home Assistant do once it knows the room?

A motion sensor knows something moved. Room presence knows which room each phone, watch and tag is in, even while you sit still. Here are eight recipes that use it, ready to paste, and four rules that stop them misfiring. We ran every one on a fresh Home Assistant 2026.11.0 with simulated room changes.

What you get to work with

In PadSpan, a device gets its Home Assistant entities when you name it. Which room is my phone in? shows how for phones and watches, and Which Bluetooth tag works? for tags. For a phone named "Alex phone" you get:

  • sensor.alex_phone_area: the room, not_home once the phone hasn't been heard for five minutes, or unknown while it has no room. Its room_confidence attribute says how sure PadSpan is. Nearly every recipe here reads this one.
  • device_tracker.alex_phone: the same room, in a tracker you can attach to a person. With no room it reads unknown, or home or not_home if you've set up the GPS Bridge.
  • sensor.alex_phone_distance: metres to the nearest scanner.

With no floor plan, each room comes from the Home Assistant area of the scanner that hears the device best, so the state is the area's own name. On a floor plan it's the name of the room you drew, normally an area too. That's why one automation can cover the whole house (recipe 4). Rooms that exist only in PadSpan (+ Add room, the Standalone layout, an imported plan) keep their own names, so recipe 4 skips them unless the name matches an area.

Change the five minutes under Settings → Tracking → Rooms and timing → Home/Away Timeout. These Settings, Manage and Mapping paths are in Advanced mode, the default. If you see Basic, use the mode button at the top right.

Using Bermuda? Its Area sensor holds the area's name too, so the room recipes carry over once you change the entity IDs. When Bermuda has no area for a device, the sensor reads unknown, not not_home, so recipe 4 won't fire on the first room after a gap.

Four rules that save you an evening

  1. Read the room from the area sensor, not the person. A person takes the strongest tracker it has. The Companion app always beats a room, and so does a router while it sees the phone connected, so person.alex just says home. When a router says the phone is gone, the person shows whichever tracker updated last, usually PadSpan's room, even for a few minutes after you've left. With PadSpan's tracker as the only one on a person, the person's state becomes the room itself, so anything waiting for home misses them, and without the GPS Bridge zone.home stops counting them too. We checked all of this on 2026.11.0. Use the Companion app for home and away. With only a router, leave PadSpan's tracker off the person.
  2. Spell the room exactly. States are case-sensitive: Living Room isn't Living room. Look yours up under Settings → Tools → States.
  3. Give it time. By default PadSpan checks every 5 seconds, smooths each scanner's readings and makes a new room win a vote, so a move takes at least 10 to 15 seconds to show, and often much longer. To see your own number, walk between two rooms with States open. Leave anything that must be instant to a motion sensor. Use for: so that walking through a doorway doesn't set everything off. If a device keeps changing rooms, see Why does my phone jump between rooms?
  4. Don't let it open anything. A phone left on the kitchen counter still reads as someone in the kitchen, and a beacon's fixed ID can be copied. Turning things off, locking up and setting the heat are fine. Don't unlock a door, open the garage or disarm an alarm on Bluetooth presence alone.

The recipes

To add an automation, go to Settings → Automations & scenes → Create automation → Create new automation, open the ⋮ menu, choose Edit in YAML and paste it over what's there. Change the names to your own, then save. Recipe 1 is different: it goes in configuration.yaml, followed by a restart. The editor's Run actions skips the trigger and the conditions, so test recipes 3, 4 and 6, which read the trigger, by walking from room to room.

1. A room sensor that knows you're still there

Motion sensors turn the lights off on someone reading a book. This sensor stays on while there's motion or a phone is in the room, and waits two minutes after both stop. Make one per room. The office here has no motion sensor, so it goes by phones alone.

template:
  - binary_sensor:
      - name: Living Room occupied
        device_class: occupancy
        delay_off: "00:02:00"
        state: >
          {{ is_state('binary_sensor.living_room_motion', 'on')
             or ['sensor.alex_phone_area', 'sensor.sam_phone_area']
                | map('states') | select('eq', 'Living Room')
                | list | count > 0 }}
      - name: Office occupied
        device_class: occupancy
        delay_off: "00:02:00"
        state: >
          {{ ['sensor.alex_phone_area', 'sensor.sam_phone_area']
             | map('states') | select('eq', 'Office')
             | list | count > 0 }}

A phone left on the sofa keeps the room on. A visitor with no phone or tag counts only while they're moving. If you already have a template: section, add these under it.

2. Lights off when the room is really empty

Your usual lights-off automation, with a better trigger. The from: "on" ignores the sensor starting up from unknown, and recipe 1's two-minute delay gives PadSpan time to find the room again after a restart.

alias: Living room lights off when it's really empty
triggers:
  - trigger: state
    entity_id: binary_sensor.living_room_occupied
    from: "on"
    to: "off"
actions:
  - action: light.turn_off
    target:
      area_id: living_room

3. Heat only the rooms in use

Ten minutes in the room turns the heat up, so walking through doesn't count. Half an hour empty turns it down. It uses the office sensor from recipe 1. The temperatures are in °C. A restart during the half hour cancels the wait, so the heat stays up until the room next empties.

alias: Office heat only while it's in use
triggers:
  - trigger: state
    entity_id: binary_sensor.office_occupied
    from: "off"
    to: "on"
    for: "00:10:00"
    id: in_use
  - trigger: state
    entity_id: binary_sensor.office_occupied
    from: "on"
    to: "off"
    for: "00:30:00"
    id: empty
actions:
  - action: climate.set_temperature
    target:
      entity_id: climate.office
    data:
      temperature: "{{ 21 if trigger.id == 'in_use' else 17 }}"

4. Lights on in whichever room you walk into

One automation for every room in the house, because the room is the name of a Home Assistant area. It lags well behind you, so use it only where there's no motion sensor and a late light is still useful. A room that isn't an area is skipped, and not_home isn't a room, so leaving the house does nothing.

alias: Lights on in whichever room Alex walks into, after dark
triggers:
  - trigger: state
    entity_id: sensor.alex_phone_area
    not_from: [unknown, unavailable]
    not_to: [unknown, unavailable, not_home]
    for: "00:00:10"
conditions:
  - condition: state
    entity_id: sun.sun
    state: below_horizon
  - condition: template
    value_template: "{{ area_id(trigger.to_state.state) is not none }}"
actions:
  - action: light.turn_on
    target:
      area_id: "{{ area_id(trigger.to_state.state) }}"

5. Say it in the room you're in

The washer finished, and only the room you're in needs to hear it. Any trigger works here. Use your own text-to-speech entity in place of tts.piper, and your own speakers. If Alex isn't in a room on the list, the kitchen hears it.

alias: Say it in the room Alex is in
triggers:
  - trigger: state
    entity_id: binary_sensor.washer_running
    from: "on"
    to: "off"
variables:
  speakers:
    Kitchen: media_player.kitchen
    Living Room: media_player.living_room
    Bedroom: media_player.bedroom
  speaker: >
    {{ speakers.get(states('sensor.alex_phone_area'), 'media_player.kitchen') }}
actions:
  - action: tts.speak
    target:
      entity_id: tts.piper
    data:
      media_player_entity_id: "{{ speaker }}"
      message: The washer is done.

6. Music that moves with you

Move from the kitchen to the office and the music comes along, even if you pass through a room with no speaker. It moves whatever is playing on those speakers, so if someone else is listening, they lose it. This needs speakers that can group in Home Assistant. Brands differ in what happens when the first speaker leaves its group, so try media_player.join and media_player.unjoin by hand under Settings → Tools → Actions first. If the music stops when the first speaker leaves, delete the unjoin step and it plays in both rooms from then on.

alias: Music moves with Alex
triggers:
  - trigger: state
    entity_id: sensor.alex_phone_area
    not_from: [unknown, unavailable, not_home]
    not_to: [unknown, unavailable, not_home]
    for: "00:00:20"
variables:
  speakers:
    Kitchen: media_player.kitchen
    Office: media_player.office
  new: "{{ speakers.get(trigger.to_state.state) }}"
  old: >
    {% set playing = speakers.values() | reject('eq', new)
       | select('is_state', 'playing') | first | default(none) %}
    {{ (state_attr(playing, 'group_members') or [playing]) | first
       if playing else none }}
conditions:
  - condition: template
    value_template: >
      {{ old is not none and new is not none
         and new not in (state_attr(old, 'group_members') or []) }}
actions:
  - action: media_player.join
    target:
      entity_id: "{{ old }}"
    data:
      group_members:
        - "{{ new }}"
  - action: media_player.unjoin
    target:
      entity_id: "{{ old }}"

7. Goodnight once everyone is in the bedroom

Both phones in the bedroom for ten minutes, late in the evening, turns off the rest of the house and locks the door. The second trigger catches phones that were already on the bedroom chargers at 21:30. If one phone charges somewhere else, it simply never fires, so use a watch or leave that person out.

alias: Goodnight once everyone is in the bedroom
triggers:
  - trigger: template
    value_template: >
      {{ is_state('sensor.alex_phone_area', 'Bedroom')
         and is_state('sensor.sam_phone_area', 'Bedroom') }}
    for: "00:10:00"
  - trigger: time
    at: "21:30:00"
conditions:
  - condition: time
    after: "21:30:00"
    before: "03:00:00"
  - condition: state
    entity_id:
      - sensor.alex_phone_area
      - sensor.sam_phone_area
    state: Bedroom
    for: "00:10:00"
actions:
  - action: light.turn_off
    target:
      area_id:
        - living_room
        - kitchen
  - action: lock.lock
    target:
      entity_id: lock.front_door

8. Tell me where the dog went

The same trigger works for a tag on a collar or a bag. Which Bluetooth tag works? has what to buy, and an automation for when you leave without your keys.

alias: The dog went into the garage
triggers:
  - trigger: state
    entity_id: sensor.dog_area
    to: Garage
    for: "00:00:30"
actions:
  - action: notify.mobile_app_alex_phone
    data:
      message: The dog is in the garage.

Less YAML? PadSpan does some of this itself

  • Follow → Movement Alerts tells you on your phone or by email when a tag changes room, arrives home or leaves.
  • Settings → Tracking → Home Assistant and integrations → Automations has simple rules: when a named device arrives or departs, turn a light, switch or script on or off, or turn on a scene.
  • Events for your own automations: padspan_device_arrived (with label and room) and padspan_device_departed (with label), for named devices.
  • Assist can answer "which room is Alex in" once you add the sentences in Getting Started.

Three more are off until you turn them on:

  • Sub-zones go finer than a room: the couch, the bed, the desk. One named Couch gets binary_sensor.padspan_couch_occupied and sensor.padspan_couch_who. They work from a device's position on the plan, which takes a measured floor plan and either three or more placed scanners hearing it, or calibration and two or more. Make a sub-zone bigger than your typical position error. Once you've computed a calibration model, Calibration shows it as Est. Accuracy, which reads better than real life, so leave a margin. Tick the line under Manage → HA Entities → Entity Publishing Controls, then draw them in Mapping → Edit → Sub-zones.
  • A head count: sensor.padspan_occupancy_occupancy estimates the people in the building, never the devices. Its rooms attribute has each room's evidence. You turn it on on the same screen, then reload the integration.
  • Guest tags: binary_sensor.padspan_guest_home is on while a tag you've lent to a visitor who agreed to carry it is heard in the house. Turn them on under Settings → Tracking → Phones and guest tags.

How we tested: on November 5, 2026 we loaded these recipes into a fresh Home Assistant 2026.11.0 as written, then ran them with their waits divided by 30. We set the sensors and device trackers by hand to stand in for PadSpan, a router and the Companion app, and used the lights, speakers, locks and heating from Home Assistant's demo integration, under the demo's own names. Recipe 5 called the demo's tts.demo_say instead of tts.speak, and in recipe 8 a notify group stood in for the phone. To make it night for recipes 4 and 7, the test server ran on Tokyo time, and for recipe 7's time trigger we set at a few seconds ahead. That shows the automations work, not how fast or how well your scanners place a phone, or how your speakers behave in a group.
