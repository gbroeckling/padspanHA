Which room is my phone in?

Your Bluetooth proxies hear your phone straight away. What they can't tell on their own is whose phone it is, because phones keep changing their Bluetooth address so that nobody can follow them. One step per phone fixes that. An Android phone sends a beacon from the Home Assistant Companion app; an iPhone or Apple Watch is recognised by its key, the IRK. From then on, Bermuda or PadSpan can put each phone in a room.

You also need at least one Bluetooth proxy, or a Shelly Gen2+ set up as a scanner. One is enough to tell home from away. For rooms, put one in every room you want to tell apart. How many Bluetooth proxies do I need? goes into the details.

Why a phone needs a step at all

Most tags and beacons keep one Bluetooth address, so a proxy knows them every time it hears them. Phones don't. They advertise from a random address and change it regularly: Android at least every 15 minutes, Apple "on a frequent basis", in its own words. That stops strangers following your phone around a shop, and it's also why Home Assistant sees your phone as a string of new devices. There are two ways around it:

  • The phone says who it is. The Android Companion app can send an iBeacon, a Bluetooth advert with an ID that never changes.
  • You hold its key. Every iPhone and Apple Watch has an Identity Resolving Key (IRK) that turns its changing addresses back into one device. Give Home Assistant the key, and it knows the phone whatever address it uses.

Android: switch on the BLE Transmitter

  1. Install the Home Assistant Companion app and sign in to your Home Assistant.
  2. In the app, go to Settings → Companion app → Manage sensors and open BLE Transmitter. Most of the app's sensors start switched off, so switch this one on.
  3. Start with its settings as they are. The app's docs warn that it can cost battery, especially with Transmit power on High, and it can be set to transmit only on your home Wi-Fi.
  4. If the phone drops off after a while, take the Companion app out of Android's battery optimisation. ESPresense's guide advises it too, because Android will otherwise stop the app.

Then name the phone in PadSpan, which is what gives it its Home Assistant entities. On PadSpan's Overview, open Track Your Phone. Your Companion app phones are listed there, and PadSpan knows each one's beacon by the ID the app reports to Home Assistant (UUID, major and minor). Select Track this phone: it takes the name of the phone's Home Assistant device and asks the app to switch its transmitter on. If the sensor is disabled in Home Assistant, the button reads Enable & Track instead; it switches the sensor back on in Home Assistant, and PadSpan then asks you to restart Home Assistant and turn the transmitter on in the app. The phone appears in its room within seconds of a proxy hearing it.

Bermuda tracks the same beacon: its README lists Android phones running the Companion app.

iPhone: get its key once

The iPhone app has no BLE Transmitter: on iOS, the Companion app only listens for iBeacons, to tell when you arrive in a zone. So an iPhone is recognised by its key instead. You get it once, in one of two ways.

  • On a Mac signed in to the same iCloud account. Open Keychain Access, find the iPhone's Bluetooth entry, and copy its Remote IRK. Home Assistant's Private BLE Device page has the steps, including how to tell which entry is your iPhone.
  • With irk-capture on a spare ESP32. irk-capture, by Derek Seaman, is an ESPHome package that makes an ESP32 look like a Bluetooth heart-rate sensor. Pair your iPhone with it in Settings → Bluetooth, and the key appears in Home Assistant as the ESP32's IRK sensor, ready to copy. That ESP32 can't be a Bluetooth proxy at the same time, so use a spare one.

Then give the key to Home Assistant, in either of two places:

  • In Home Assistant: Settings → Devices & services → Add integration → Private BLE Device, and paste it. Hex and base64 both work.
  • In PadSpan: Settings → Presence → Phone Tracking (Private BLE) → Add IRK. PadSpan listens for up to 30 seconds to check that the key matches a phone your proxies can hear, then adds the Private BLE Device entry to Home Assistant for you. (From the 0.38.202 beta, Presence is called Tracking.)

Either way, keep the iPhone awake, with Bluetooth on, near a proxy while you do it: Home Assistant only accepts a key once it can hear the phone it belongs to. Adding a key needs a Home Assistant administrator. PadSpan reloads its list of keys every five minutes, so a key added in Home Assistant reaches it within about five minutes. Bermuda gives every Private BLE Device its sensors automatically.

Last, name the iPhone in PadSpan, which is what gives it its Home Assistant entities. On Overview, open All objects in the Objects card, select Tag on the iPhone's row, type a name and select Save label. On a new install, before you've drawn any rooms, the Who's where now card has a Track button beside the iPhone's name that does the same.

Installed PadSpan before version 0.38.165? Then Overview → Track Your Phone shows iPhones the Android steps, unless you switch on Settings → Features → iPhone Setup Path (experimental; newer installs start with it on). The steps on this page work either way.

Apple Watch

An Apple Watch has its own key, separate from its iPhone's, and the same two ways get it. Home Assistant's Keychain steps are written for "your iPhone or Apple Watch", and irk-capture lists Apple Watches among the devices it was tested with: pair the watch from its own Settings → Bluetooth. Then add the key to Private BLE Device as an entry of its own, and tag the watch in PadSpan the same way.

What you get

  • In PadSpan: each named phone shows in its room on a floor plan of your house, and the Overview's People in building card lists who's home and the room each person is in. An Android phone counts as a person there when Home Assistant shows that person as home and the phone's PadSpan name matches the person's name or one of their trackers.
  • In Home Assistant: once a phone has a name in PadSpan, it gets a device tracker whose state is the room it's in (or away, once it hasn't been heard for five minutes, by default), an Area sensor and Distance sensors. Link the tracker to a person under Settings → People and the person shows the room too. One catch: if that person also has another tracker, such as the Companion app's GPS or a router's, Home Assistant goes by that one and shows them as home, not in a room.
  • Private BLE Device on its own gives home or away and an estimated distance to the nearest proxy, not a room. Bermuda and PadSpan add the room.
  • Alerts: PadSpan's Follow screen has Movement Alerts for when a phone changes room.

Phones you haven't set up

A phone without a key or a beacon is nameless, not invisible. PadSpan counts it as an unknown phone in its room in People in building when at least two proxies hear it, one of them strongly, and its advert is a kind iPhones and Apple Watches send (such as Nearby Info or Handoff) or comes from a wearable such as a Garmin, Fitbit, Polar, Oura or Withings. If someone is home but not placed in a room, PadSpan assumes it's them and says so. These phones get no entities and no names; for that, give them their step above.

When a phone won't show up

  • "The provided IRK does not match any BLE devices that Home Assistant can see." Wake the phone, bring it near a proxy and try again.
  • "The key does not look like a valid IRK." Copy the whole key: 32 hex characters, or the base64 text that ends in "=".
  • An Android phone disappears after a while. Battery optimisation has usually stopped the Companion app; see step 4 above.
  • irk-capture can't get a key from a watch. Its docs say watches that need "reverse" pairing won't work, and some watches, including some Garmins, keep a fixed Bluetooth address and have no IRK. A watch with a fixed address doesn't need one: it can be tracked like a tag.
  • A phone keeps jumping between rooms. That's signal noise, not setup: Why does my phone jump between rooms? explains what helps. And if whole rooms never show up, How many Bluetooth proxies do I need?
