Bermuda, NeonPlan 3D or PadSpan?

All three tell you which room each phone, watch and tag is in, and all three are in Home Assistant 2026.11's new Marketplace. The Marketplace won't show you they do the same job. Search "bluetooth" and Bermuda comes first, search "floor plan" and NeonPlan 3D is near the top, and search "room presence" and PadSpan comes first. This is what each one does, from their own docs as of November 2026, and which fits which house. PadSpan's developer wrote it, so the sources are at the end.

The short version

  • Bermuda if sensors for your automations are all you want, from the proxies you already have. It's the most widely used of the three, there's very little to set up, and it has no map.
  • NeonPlan 3D if a free 3D house you can look at and control is what you're after, and its free Locating also tells you who's in which room. You set up its rooms first (it can start them from your areas), then walk each room for 90 seconds.
  • PadSpan if knowing where everyone is comes first. It works from each scanner's Home Assistant area before you've drawn anything, and it can alert you when a device changes room. A floor plan adds movement playback and, with its scale set, a calibration walk and positions between scanners. It runs next to Bermuda, so you don't have to choose. Exact placement on its Atlas map and the 3D house (still experimental) are Pro.

Bermuda

Bermuda turns the Bluetooth adverts your proxies already hear into Home Assistant sensors: which area each device is in, its distance from the nearest proxy, and a device tracker. It recognises iPhones and Apple Watches through Home Assistant's Private BLE Device, and iBeacons even when their address changes, which covers Android phones running the Home Assistant app. Its calibration is guided, with settings for the whole house, each proxy and each device. Its wiki says a map of your house "is not yet a thing" and names one as a goal, so what you get today is sensors for the areas your proxies are in, plus a Floor sensor.

Good fit: you already have Bluetooth proxies (ESPHome or Shelly), and sensors for your automations are all you want.

NeonPlan 3D

NeonPlan 3D began as a 3D floor plan, and that's its main job. You draw your house in its editor, furniture included, and see and control lights, blinds, climate, media and cameras in a neon 3D view, as a sidebar panel or a dashboard card that runs on old wall tablets. Since version 2.0 it also locates people, for free. You set each scanner's room and add your devices, and its key helper gets the key from an iPhone or Apple Watch with a cheap ESP32 and no Mac. Then you walk each room for 90 seconds with a phone in your pocket. People stand in their rooms as glowing figures, each device gets a room sensor, and Assist can tell you where your iPhone is and ring it. Motion and presence sensors speed up room changes. Its manual says plainly that it works room by room, not to the centimetre, and that AirTags don't work.

Good fit: you want a 3D house first, for free, perhaps on a wall tablet, and knowing the room is enough.

PadSpan

PadSpan starts from the other end, with where people are. It reads Home Assistant's own Bluetooth data, so the ESPHome proxies and Shelly scanners the other two use serve it as well. Rooms come from each scanner's Home Assistant area, so with a scanner in each room it works with no plan and no walk. When you want more, you trace your rooms on a picture of your plan, or use your robot vacuum's map (experimental), and see everyone on it, one floor at a time or all floors stacked. On a plan with its scale set, a walk around the house teaches it what each room's signals look like, and with three or more scanners placed on a measured plan it can put a device between them, and in a room you've drawn that has no scanner of its own. It smooths readings with a Kalman filter and makes a new room win a vote before it moves anyone.

On a floor plan, Traceback plays back where each device went. Follow can alert you when one changes room, and guest tags let you lend a tag to a visitor who has agreed to carry it. All of that is free. It wants more than 2 GB of RAM on your Home Assistant machine (a 2 GB machine can turn on Low-memory mode).

PadSpan Pro adds exact placement on the Atlas, the touchscreen map of the whole house with every light and device where it really is, and Live Aboard, your house in 3D like looking into a house in The Sims (still experimental), with Furnish. It also adds wall mode for a big touch screen, and Forensics, Busy Times and Locate. You can try all of Pro free for 90 days, no card. The trial needs an email address and the opt-in usage report on, and the report stays on until the trial ends.

Good fit: where people are matters most to you. You want to start without drawing anything, tune it on a real plan, see where someone's phone went, and be told when it moves.

Can I run them together?

Yes. Each one reads what Home Assistant's Bluetooth already hears, and none of them takes over your scanners, so the easiest test is your own house. Install two and keep the one you use. We installed all three from the Marketplace on the same 2026.11 beta on November 4, with no errors and no restart. That test had no scanners attached, so it says nothing about how well they locate side by side.

By default, devices Bermuda tracks show up in PadSpan in the room Bermuda's Area sensor reports; Bermuda and PadSpan together explains how. NeonPlan 3D doesn't read Bermuda or PadSpan, so its figures always show its own Locating.

Installing any of them

Go to Settings → Marketplace, search for the name and press Install. The first time, the Marketplace asks you to agree to a warning that what it lists is made by community contributors and isn't reviewed by the Home Assistant project. A setup dialog follows. For PadSpan's first steps, see From the Marketplace to your first room.

ESPresense isn't in the Marketplace, because it's firmware for ESP32 boards; Bermuda, ESPresense or PadSpan? covers it. NextFloor, a free fork of NeonPlan 3D, doesn't locate people, so Which floor plan for Home Assistant? covers it with the other floor plans.
