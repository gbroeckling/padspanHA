Your Shellys can be Bluetooth proxies

About one in five Home Assistant installs that share analytics has the Shelly integration. If you have Shelly Plus, Pro, Gen3 or Gen4 devices on mains power, each one can be a Bluetooth scanner for Home Assistant. It's one setting per device, and a free way to start room presence before you buy or flash anything.

See room presence on a demo house Open PadSpan in HACS

Which Shellys can do it

  • Gen2 and newer, on mains power. That means the Plus and Pro models and anything with Gen3 or Gen4 in its name. In Home Assistant's words: "Shelly Gen 2 and newer devices (excluding battery-powered models) can act as Bluetooth proxies, forwarding advertisement data."
  • Not battery-powered ones, and not Gen1.
  • Shelly BLU devices (buttons, door and window sensors, H&T) are Bluetooth sensors, not scanners. Home Assistant reads them with its BTHome integration.

Switch it on

  1. In Home Assistant, go to Settings → Devices & services → Shelly.
  2. Each Shelly has its own entry. Select the cogwheel on the one you want.
  3. Set Bluetooth scanner mode to Auto, which Home Assistant recommends for most setups, and select Submit. Active and Passive work too.
  4. Check that the Shelly's device is in the right area: the room it's really in. PadSpan uses each scanner's Home Assistant area as its room.

Do the same for every Shelly you want to use.

What PadSpan does with them

  • It finds them by itself. PadSpan reads Home Assistant's own Bluetooth data, so it uses a Shelly scanner as soon as Home Assistant does, next to any ESPHome proxies, with nothing to add. Once the Shelly has an area, PadSpan's Health tab lists it under Scanner → Room Mappings.
  • Its room is its area. PadSpan finds each scanner's Home Assistant device and takes that device's area as the scanner's room. With a scanner in each room you want told apart, every phone, watch and tag goes to the room whose scanner hears it best.
  • You can mix Shellys and ESP32 proxies. Different radios hear the same phone at different strengths, so in a mixed house the calibration walk matters more: it lets PadSpan allow for each scanner on its own.
  • Bermuda uses them too. Its own docs list Shelly Gen2+ devices, so one setting serves both. See Bermuda and PadSpan together.

How well it works

A Shelly sits in a wall box, often behind a switch, with a small built-in antenna. That can make it hear less, and less steadily, than a proxy out in the room with an external antenna, which is why How many Bluetooth proxies do I need? recommends boards with one. So treat your Shellys as a free start:

  • One is enough for home or away.
  • Whether they're good enough for rooms depends on your house. Calibration → Model shows how often PadSpan picks the right room on spots it wasn't calibrated on (from version 0.38.166). The rooms that keep scoring badly are where an ESP32 proxy goes.
  • Shellys only listen; they can't hold a Bluetooth connection. Presence only needs listening, but a device Home Assistant has to connect to needs a proxy that can.

A Shelly that hears nothing, or has no room

  • It shows up without a room although its device has an area? Update to PadSpan 0.38.175 or later (a beta for now). Home Assistant gives a Shelly's scanner the Shelly's Bluetooth address, not the one its device is filed under, so earlier versions found the device only when the names lined up. 0.38.175 finds it by address.
  • Check that its Bluetooth scanner mode isn't Disabled and that its firmware is up to date. If it still hears nothing, try another mode, such as Passive.
  • If it hears devices but PadSpan's calibration shows no signal strengths for it, open Manage → HA Entities → BLE Proxy Compatibility in PadSpan and tick Enable aggressive BLE reseed (5s) for passive proxy scanners. It works around a change in Home Assistant 2026.4 that holds back repeat readings from scanners that only listen.

Phones need one more step

Scanners hear tags and beacons straight away. Phones change their Bluetooth address so they can't be followed: iPhones and Apple Watches need their key (the IRK), and Android phones need the Home Assistant Companion app's BLE Transmitter. How many Bluetooth proxies do I need? has the details.
