From the Marketplace to your first room

Home Assistant 2026.11 makes installing PadSpan a search and a click. This page is what comes after that: give each Bluetooth scanner its room, see who's where without drawing anything, name your phone and, if you want it, put your house on a map. All of that is free, and the steps that are easy to miss are called out.

See a new install's first screen Install PadSpan

What you need

  • Home Assistant. The Marketplace comes with 2026.11. PadSpan itself runs on 2024.1 or newer, installed through HACS on anything before 2026.11.
  • At least one Bluetooth scanner. An ESPHome Bluetooth proxy, a Shelly Gen2 or newer set up as a scanner, or your Home Assistant machine's own Bluetooth. One scanner tells home from away. For rooms, put one in every room you want to tell apart: How many Bluetooth proxies do I need?
  • An area for each room. PadSpan's rooms are Home Assistant's areas, under Settings → Areas, labels & zones.
  • More than 2 GB of RAM is recommended. Near a busy street PadSpan hears thousands of passing devices, and a 2 GB Raspberry Pi can run out of memory within hours. On a small machine, PadSpan's own Settings → Presence has a Low-memory mode.

No scanner yet? You can install PadSpan anyway. It shows a made-up house until it hears its first scanner, then switches to yours by itself. Or try the demo in your browser, with nothing to install.

1. Install it

On Home Assistant 2026.11 or newer:

  1. Open Settings → Marketplace. The first time, Home Assistant warns you that community projects aren't reviewed by the Home Assistant project. It can't install anything until you accept.
  2. Search for room presence or PadSpan, open PadSpan HA and select Install. Check that the dialog offers 0.38.199 or newer, then select Install again.
  3. The setup dialog opens straight away, with no restart and nothing to fill in. Select Submit.
  4. Open PadSpan HA in the sidebar. The other new entry, Atlas, is the map of your lights and devices. Leave it for later.

On 2026.10 or older, install PadSpan HA from HACS, restart Home Assistant fully, then add it under Settings → Devices & services → Add integration. The Install PadSpan button on this page opens the right place on either. Already have PadSpan through HACS? Upgrading to 2026.11 moves it to the Marketplace by itself, and it keeps getting updates.

2. Give every scanner its room

PadSpan puts each device in the room whose scanner hears it best, and a scanner's room is the Home Assistant area of its device. A scanner with no area gives no room, however well it hears you: a phone right beside it lands in the next scanner's room. So this comes first.

You can set the area on the device in Home Assistant, under Settings → Devices & services → Devices. Or do it in PadSpan: on the Overview, in the Bluetooth radios card, select View radios list, then Assign beside the scanner, pick the area and select Save. A Shelly's scanner is a device of its own in Home Assistant, and the Shelly guide covers that.

3. See who's where

You don't need a floor plan for this. On the Overview, the Who's where now card lists each room, the named devices in it, and a count of everything else its scanner hears.

The screen tells you where you stand:

4. Name your phone

A device you haven't named gets no entities from PadSpan. Naming one gives it a device tracker whose state is the room, an Area sensor and Distance sensors.

  • Android: switch on BLE Transmitter in the Companion app (Manage sensors). Then on the Overview, under Track Your Phone, select Track this phone. If it shows Setup or Enable & Track, the sensor isn't on yet, and that button says what to do.
  • iPhone or Apple Watch: get its key, the IRK, into Home Assistant once, then select Track beside its name in Who's where now. It can take five minutes to appear, and once rooms are on the map that card is gone, so use All objects → Tag instead. Which room is my phone in? has the steps.
  • A beacon or key tag: on the Overview, in the Objects card, open All objects, select Tag on its row, type a name and select Save label. Which Bluetooth tag works? has what to buy.

5. Put your house on a map

This part is optional, and it's free. It turns the list of rooms into your house, drawn on the Overview. Calibration needs it, and with three or more scanners placed on it, PadSpan can also place devices between them, even in rooms with no scanner of their own.

Select Make it a map on the Who's where now card. The wizard has five steps, one floor at a time:

  1. Upload floor plan. Any picture of the floor works, even a photo of a sketch (PNG, JPG, WebP, GIF or SVG). Choose its floor, give it a name and select Upload & Convert.
  2. Set the scale. Click both ends of a wall you know, type its length in metres and select Add 1st Measurement. Do the same along a wall at a different angle, ideally at right angles to the first, and select Add 2nd Measurement. Then select Save Scale. It only takes metres, so 12 feet is 3.66. A sketch may show a warning that the map appears stretched, and you can still save. Don't skip this one: without a scale, nothing can go on the map at the end, and calibration can't start.
  3. Draw rooms. Under the map, pick a room from Choose room… (the list is your Home Assistant areas), select Start drawing, click each corner, then select Finish or double-click. When the floor's rooms are drawn, select Save Layout, because Next doesn't save for you. A room missing from the list? Check under Settings → Areas, labels & zones that its area is on this floor.
  4. Place scanners. In the Live BLE Radios list under the map, select Add beside each scanner on this floor, drag it to where it's mounted, and select Save Layout. Use Add in this list, not Add Receiver above it or a double-click on the map, even though the step's own text suggests that: only a marker from this list is linked to a real scanner.
  5. Finish, then Put it on the map. This is the step that's easy to miss. Until you select Put it on the map, your rooms and scanners are only on the picture, and Done doesn't move them. Once the first room is on, your house takes the place of Who's where now on the Overview, for the whole house, so draw every room your scanners cover: a device in a room you haven't drawn may not show. For the next floor, select + Set up another floor and set its scale too, even though the step says the scale is set. With two floors or more, line them up and set their heights in Mapping → 3D Stack.

Already finished, and the Overview says "Positioning fabric not built yet"? You don't have to start again, and you don't have to go to Mapping → Rooms, where that message sends you. Select Make it a map, then 5. Finish, then Put it on the map. That covers your first plan. For another floor, use Commit this layout to fabric in Mapping → Rooms and Calibration → Tune → Save. A scanner you placed with a double-click needs adding again from Live BLE Radios first. Versions before 0.38.174 don't have the button, so update PadSpan first.

No picture of the floor? In Mapping → Rooms you can add rooms in metres with no picture: type the area's exact name in the New room name box, select + Add room, then drag it to shape and select Save corrections. Calibration still needs a plan with a scale. The same screen imports a Sweet Home 3D file, and a robot vacuum's map can give you the plan and rooms at once on an empty floor: turn on PadSpan's Settings → Features → Robot Vacuum Import first (experimental, off by default). Which floor plan for Home Assistant? compares your options.

6. Calibrate

The strongest scanner is a good first answer. Calibration teaches PadSpan what each spot in your house really sounds like, and it's free. It needs a floor with a plan and a scale. Open Calibration in PadSpan's menu, select Guided calibration, choose your phone or a tag and the floor, walk the house, then select Compute Model. PadSpan listens at each spot: 15 seconds is the default, the panel recommends 60, and it asks for 10–20 spots spread across each floor.

Open it yourself rather than waiting for the checklist: the Overview's checklist counts a scanner on the map as calibrated, so it closes itself after Put it on the map, before you've walked anywhere. If a phone still jumps between rooms, see Why does my phone jump between rooms?

Free, and what Pro adds

Everything on this page is free. PadSpan Pro adds exact placement on the Atlas, the touchscreen map of the whole house, so every light and device sits where it really is; Live Aboard, the same house in 3D, like looking into a house in The Sims (still experimental); Furnish, for your furniture; the wall screen; and Forensics, Busy Times and Locate. You can try all of Pro free for 90 days, no card. Start it from PadSpan's Settings → Features → PadSpan licence, or from the last row of the Overview's checklist while that's showing. It asks for an email to send your key and needs the usage report on, which can't be turned off until the trial ends.

If it still doesn't work

  • "Bluetooth feed unavailable". Restart Home Assistant fully, under Settings → System → Restart. The panel says a reload isn't enough after a first install.
  • Uploading a plan, saving the scale or starting the trial is refused. Those need a Home Assistant administrator.
  • Calibration has gone from the menu, or Mapping has lost Edit and Rooms. The mode button at the top right has switched the panel to Basic. Select it again and it goes back to Advanced.
  • All objects is full of strangers' devices. Normal near a street. In that list, change All statuses to Identified, or search by name.
  • Something else. Open an issue on GitHub.
