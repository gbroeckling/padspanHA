Where is everyone? The whole house, live, on one wall

In a five-floor house, nobody knows where anyone is. PadSpan puts the answer on the wall: every floor, every room, and every phone, watch and tagged set of keys you've set up, live on one touch screen. Big houses are where it's landing. Of the 58 homes that sent PadSpan's opt-in usage report between October 12 and 26, 2026, 16 have three floors or more, and one has five floors and 34 rooms.

What's on the wall

See it for yourself: the demo house as a wall screen, in your browser.

  • Every floor. All floors stacked top to bottom, or one floor at a time. With Show each floor on, the wall steps through them every 15 seconds to 5 minutes, and waits while someone is using it.
  • Everyone in their room. With Show people and Show tags & scanners on, each person, phone, watch and tag PadSpan follows shows in its room, updated every few seconds.
  • The house itself. Doors, windows, blinds, garage doors and gates on the map, once linked. With Alert pulse on, a door left open for hours, or a window open while it rains, rings its room in amber; leak, smoke, gas and CO alarms always ring it in red.
  • What just happened. In the 3D house (Live Aboard), the view can fly to a doorbell that rings, a door that opens or an alarm that goes off, show a card with a camera's snapshot if you picked one, and fly back home.

Reads from across the room

  • Sized for the room it hangs in. Give the screen's size and how far away people stand, and PadSpan sizes the text by how big it looks from there, the rule control rooms use. A 55-inch screen across a room gets much bigger text than a 27-inch monitor on a desk, even when both are 4K.
  • 4K-ready. On a landscape 4K screen everything draws at twice its 1080p size, so it reads from across the room.
  • Quiet when nobody is looking. The controls over the map fade 30 seconds after the last touch, the wall can dim between sunset and sunrise, and optional burn-in protection shifts the picture two pixels every three minutes for OLED and plasma screens.
  • Every screen its own. Each wall can have its own size and its own link, and a wall reloads itself after an update, so it doesn't keep running old code. With a Fully PLUS licence, the Fully Kiosk option and Fully's JavaScript Interface on, night dimming turns the tablet's own brightness down, and with Fully's Motion Detection (it needs a front camera), walking up wakes it.

The Wall Screen Guide has every setting, and Which touch screen for a PadSpan wall? covers the screens themselves.

Scanners for a big house

  • One Bluetooth proxy in each room you want told apart gives you rooms. Three or more on a floor, placed on a measured plan, let PadSpan put a device between them. Each floor counts on its own. How many Bluetooth proxies do I need?
  • Some may already be in the walls. Shelly Gen2+ relays can be Bluetooth scanners with one setting each, as a free start: in a wall box they hear less than a proxy in the room. Your Shellys can be Bluetooth proxies.
  • No floor plans yet? If your robot vacuum runs Valetudo, or has a map camera with calibration points, the experimental Robot Vacuum Import (Settings → Features) can make its map the floor's plan: Your robot vacuum already drew your floor plan.

Private, and ready for the other houses

  • It runs on your own Home Assistant. Room presence, the maps and the movement history stay in the house, and PadSpan works offline. A daily update check sends the version (it can be turned off); with a key, a daily licence check sends the key, Home Assistant's instance ID and this home's licence details. The usage report is opt-in, but a key on more than two homes needs it.
  • One licence, up to five homes. One PadSpan Pro key covers up to five Home Assistants: the first two always, a third to fifth while every home on the key keeps PadSpan's usage report on.
  • It works next to what already runs the house. PadSpan is a Home Assistant integration, so the lights, locks, doors and sensors Home Assistant already has show in their rooms.

Free and Pro

Room presence, the floor plans, calibration and the Home Assistant sensors are free and open source (GPL-3.0). The wall mode, the 3D house (Live Aboard, still experimental) and placing lights and other devices exactly on the map are part of PadSpan Pro (PadSpan's main page also has a free ?kiosk=1 link for a tablet). You can try all of Pro free for 90 days in Settings → Features → PadSpan licence, no card (from 0.38.180, the trial needs the opt-in usage report on). See Pro on the main page.
