26. Troubleshooting by symptom

Contents

Find the symptom, look where it says first, then work down the causes. The screens are named as the panel names them; some are under More tools at the bottom of the menu (in Basic mode, most of them are).

Two places help with almost everything:

  • The status dot in the top bar shows only when something is wrong: amber needs a look, red is broken. Hovering over it names the problems; clicking it opens Health.
  • Health → Problems found checks the whole setup for what puts devices in the wrong room, and each card ends with an "Action:" line saying what to do.

If none of this helps, chapter 27 says what to send.

A device shows the wrong room, or jumps between rooms

Look first: the Receivers page (is every receiver heard, and in the right room?), then Health → Problems found (a ROOMS MIXED UP card names the two rooms it keeps moving between).

Likely causes, most common first:

  1. A receiver is silent or hearing less. Rooms near it go wrong until it recovers. See A receiver is silent below.
  2. A receiver is in the wrong room. PadSpan places a device in the room of the receiver that hears it best, so a receiver given the wrong room sends devices there. Fix it in Overview → View radios list (Assign or Change), or in Mapping → Edit → Receivers.
  3. Two rooms' receivers hear the device almost the same. Near a doorway, two receivers can be only a few dB apart. Put each receiver well inside its room, nearer the middle than a corner, not in a cabinet, behind metal or against an outside wall. A room with no receiver of its own can only be told apart with calibration, and less reliably.
  4. No calibration yet, or old calibration. Calibration corrects the nearest-receiver room. Start 🧭 Guided calibration in Calibration; add points in both rooms near where they meet. Calibration → Model shows how often PadSpan picks the right room on spots it was not calibrated on.
  5. A receiver that reads stronger or weaker than the rest. Give it a trim in Settings → Tracking → Receivers → Receiver signal adjustments (or on the Receivers page).

Then, if it still flickers on a boundary:

  • Raise Room Change Delay (Settings → Tracking → Rooms and timing; 40 seconds unless you changed it). 40 to 60 seconds steadies most boundaries; a longer delay is steadier and slower.
  • Turn on Why This Room (Sensor Attribute) in the same section. The area sensor then says, in its room_reason attribute, how many polls voted for the room and why a move was held or kept (chapter 14).
  • Draw a signal wall between the two rooms in Mapping → Edit if there is a real wall there.

A device that has gone quiet keeps the room it was last in (since 0.38.271): it no longer drifts to another room, or another floor, on old readings. A pinned beacon reports its room once, and a room change is never sent twice running. More on how PadSpan keeps rooms steady: Why does my phone jump between rooms?

A device goes away too soon, or stays too long

Look first: Settings → Tracking → Rooms and timing → Home/Away Timeout.

  • not_home too soon: a device unheard for the away timeout (5 minutes unless you changed it) reads not_home. For a device that is briefly out of range in a thick-walled room, raise it to 10 to 30 minutes. For keys and tags nobody carries, Things keep their room lets them stay in their last room for hours instead.
  • not_home too late: lower the timeout. The shortest is 1 minute.
  • The room it was left in: every tracker and area sensor has last_room, which stays after it reads not_home.

A device doesn't show up

Look first: the Receivers page (is any receiver reporting?), then Bluetooth → Scanners (what each one hears).

  1. No receiver is reporting. The status dot is red. See the next sections.
  2. The device isn't sending, or is out of range. It has to be within range of at least one receiver and advertising over Bluetooth. Wake it, bring it next to a receiver, and look again.
  3. It is there but hidden. Quiet Mode (the Quiet tick at the top of the Overview) hides every device nobody has named. Near a street, Overview's All objects is full of strangers' devices: change All statuses to Identified, or search by name.
  4. It changed its address. Phones, watches and Find My tags change their Bluetooth address, so an unnamed one shows as a new device each time. A phone needs its key (next section). For a named AirTag or other Find My tag, turn on MAC Rotation Bridging in Settings → Features.
  5. An iBeacon splits into several devices. Your receivers must pass on the whole advertisement (company 0x004C with an iBeacon payload). A tracker that changes its own UUID can't be followed by it.
  6. No Home Assistant entity for it. PadSpan makes entities only for devices you named, and only the kinds ticked in Manage → HA Entities → Entity Publishing Controls.

A phone isn't recognised, or shows as many devices

Look first: Bluetooth → IRK Manager.

  • An iPhone or Apple Watch shows as many unknown devices. Without its identity key (IRK), every address change looks like a new device. Add the key in Bluetooth → IRK Manager → Add an IRK; Test only checks a key without saving it. A key added in PadSpan works at once. One added in Home Assistant's Private BLE Device is picked up within five minutes.
  • "The provided IRK does not match any BLE devices that Home Assistant can see." Wake the phone, bring it near a receiver, and try again.
  • "The key does not look like a valid IRK." Copy the whole key: 32 hex characters, or the base64 text that ends in "=".
  • An Android phone says "Waiting for signal". Its Companion app's BLE Transmitter is off. In the Home Assistant app on the phone, open Settings → Companion app → Manage sensors → BLE Transmitter and switch it on. Make sure the phone's Bluetooth is on, then walk near a receiver.
  • An Android phone says "BLE off". The command to switch the transmitter on didn't reach the app. Switch it on by hand as above; the phone must be connected to your Home Assistant for the command to work.
  • An Android phone is heard only right next to a receiver, or disappears after a while. Its transmitter is on its weakest settings, or battery saving has stopped the Companion app. The phone guide has the steps: iPhone, Apple Watch and Android room presence.

A receiver is silent

Look first: the Receivers page's Status column, and Health. A receiver shows Silent … min (the minutes since it was last heard) once nothing has come from it for over 10 minutes, or Hearing less when it hears far fewer devices than usual while the others are fine.

  1. Restart it. On ESPHome, also check its version and its Wi-Fi.
  2. Check it is still a receiver in Home Assistant: Settings → Devices & services → its integration (ESPHome, Shelly and so on). A proxy that lost its Bluetooth setting stops reporting.
  3. A passive receiver (Shelly, an ESPHome proxy set to passive) heard only now and then: turn on Refresh every 5 s for passive proxy scanners in Settings → Tracking → Receivers.
  4. A receiver you took away for good: choose Mark as Lost in its details, or Exclude it in Bluetooth → Scanners, so it stops counting as silent. Either keeps what it recorded, and Restore Radio or Include brings it back.

A receiver you marked lost, disabled or excluded never lights the status dot.

"Room and receiver layout not built yet"

The Overview says this when your floor plans have rooms or receivers on them but PadSpan's map of the house (in metres) has none: devices are placed by nearest receiver only, and nothing is drawn on the house.

  • Press Put it on the map (the last step of Setup, and on each room in Rooms), or in Mapping → Layout, Commit this layout.
  • Health → Problems found → FLOOR PLAN says when no plan has its scale set yet: a plan needs its scale before anything can be placed in metres.

The panel isn't updating

Look first: the bar at the top of the panel, and the status dot.

  • "PadSpan was updated to v…. This page is still running v…": press Reload.
  • "This page is v…, but Home Assistant is running PadSpan v…. If you just updated, restart Home Assistant.": restart Home Assistant (Settings → System → Restart), then reload.
  • "Bluetooth feed unavailable" after a first install: restart Home Assistant fully. Reloading the integration isn't enough the first time.
  • A red status dot: PadSpan isn't answering, positioning stopped, or no receiver is reporting. Hover over it to see which; Health has the detail.
  • Changes you made don't show: hard refresh the page (Ctrl+F5, or Cmd+Shift+R on a Mac). Health → System shows the panel's version and build, and Diagnostics → Install Verification compares it with Home Assistant's. If they differ, Home Assistant is serving an older copy: look for two copies of PadSpan in custom_components, remove one, and restart.
  • Mapping has only Library and Upload, or Settings has no tabs: the panel is in Basic mode. The mode button at the top right switches back to Advanced.
  • A button is refused: uploading a plan, setting the scale, backups and anything that sends data out need a Home Assistant administrator, and with Enhanced security on, so does any change to the shared setup.

Alerts aren't sending

Look first: Follow, the alert settings for that device, and Send Test.

  1. Nothing was saved. Tick what you want to hear about, then Save: nothing is active until it is saved.
  2. No way to send. Alerts go out through Home Assistant's notify services. For a phone, the Companion app must be set up; for email, a mail integration such as SMTP (Settings → Devices & services). Send Test says plainly if it couldn't reach your phone or mail service.
  3. Too close together. A device's room alerts are at least 60 seconds apart; a change inside that time is not sent on its own.
  4. Arrive and leave alerts come late or early: they follow the away timeout, as not_home does (above).
  5. Your own automation never fires: PadSpan's events fire only for named devices, and padspan_room_change only for followed devices with Room-change tag events on (Settings → Tracking → Home Assistant and integrations → HA Tags Integration). See chapter 14.

The 3D house won't start

Look first: the Atlas. When Live Aboard can't run, the Atlas shows the flat map instead, and the 🏠 button says why; tap it to try again.

  • No WebGL, too slow, or the graphics restarted: the browser or the device can't run the 3D view. Try Quality low (Settings → Atlas → Live Aboard), another browser, or another device. A wall tablet may manage only the flat Atlas.
  • No 3D switch at all: Live Aboard is a Pro feature, and it is off unless Show Live Aboard on the Atlas is ticked in Settings → Atlas.
  • A device floats in the wrong place: set its height in Live Aboard's Edit → Heights, or move it on the map.
  • A room's temperature shows "–°": its sensor hasn't reported for over an hour.

Home Assistant runs out of memory, or is slow

Look first: how much memory the machine has. Next to a busy street or in a block of flats, PadSpan hears thousands of passing devices, and on a 2 GB machine Home Assistant has run out of memory within hours.

  1. On a 2 GB machine, turn on Low-memory mode (Settings → Tracking → Performance). It keeps fewer passing devices nobody named. Your named, tagged and followed devices are never dropped.
  2. Keep unnamed devices for less time: Settings → Tracking → What the panel shows → Object History Retention, 1 day (the default).
  3. Several wall screens: turn on Optimize for scale (Settings → Tracking → Performance).
  4. The room work slows Home Assistant: CPU Mode (same section) can hold it to one core.
  5. Move to a machine with more memory: chapter 22 says how, and Minimum requirements has the figures.
