1. What PadSpan is

Contents

PadSpan HA is a control panel for your house, inside Home Assistant. It draws your floors and rooms, puts each light, lock and sensor in its room, and, with Bluetooth receivers, shows which room each person is in. A tap on a light switches it. A leak or smoke alarm turns its room red, and a red banner shows it on every PadSpan screen. It can be as simple or complex as you need: start with your rooms and their lights, add receivers for who's where, and add PadSpan Pro when you want more.

PadSpan has two pages in Home Assistant's sidebar. PadSpan HA is the whole panel: who is where, Follow for one phone or tag, a replay of where devices went, setup and settings. Atlas is the map of your house, made for a phone or a screen on the wall. PadSpan runs on your Home Assistant, and the tracking needs no cloud.

You can try it before installing: padspan.traks.ca/demo is the real panel on a sample house, in your browser. The demo shows PadSpan Pro, so it includes the paid parts.

What you get for free

PadSpan HA is free and open source (GPL v3). The free version needs no key and doesn't run out. Some features need PadSpan Pro or an add-on, and PadSpan says which where you meet them. Pro includes every add-on. With no key, you get everything below.

Your house on one screen

These need only Home Assistant, with no Bluetooth receiver.

  • The Atlas: your floors and rooms, with every light and fan in its room and a tap to switch it. Tap a room's name for its sheet: every device in it, All lights on / off, and a button for each of the room's scenes and scripts in Home Assistant (up to six). See chapter 15.
  • Locks on the map, with Lock and Unlock in their card. Unlock asks first.
  • Leak, smoke, gas and CO alarms ring their room red and stay alarming for 2 days, or until someone resets them. A red banner shows them on every PadSpan screen, and you get one notification when one goes off and one when it clears (chapter 13).
  • A house alarm going off (an alarm panel in Home Assistant, such as Alarmo) shows on the Atlas's emergency button and as a row in the red banner.
  • Alert pulse (off until you turn it on): a door, window, gate or cover open for more than 6 hours, or a window or cover open in the rain or snow, rings its room amber.
  • Rain and snow round the house on the Atlas, drawn still, from your weather entity or a rain sensor.
  • Open screens (off until you turn it on): every open PadSpan page in one list, to reload one or all, and, from an automation, a message with a chime on the screens you name (chapter 14).
  • A kiosk link, /padspan-ha?kiosk=1, hides PadSpan's menu for a tablet (chapter 18). Wall mode on the Atlas page is PadSpan Pro's.
  • The Night, Light and High contrast themes. The panel speaks your Home Assistant language: English and German, and much of it French and Dutch.

Your lights

These need only Home Assistant too.

  • Every light on the Atlas, in its room, with a tap to switch it (above).
  • Power teams: a wall dimmer or switch and the smart bulbs it feeds become one light in Home Assistant. It follows the day, can wake you, and sets the bulbs again when the wall switch turns them back on (chapter 15).

Who's in which room

These need at least one Bluetooth receiver. One tells home from away; one in each room you want told apart gives you rooms.

  • Rooms with no floor plan. Each receiver's room is its Home Assistant area. A device goes to the room whose receiver hears it best. The Overview's Who's where now card lists each room and who is in it. Set room gives a receiver with no area its room, and Track starts tracking a phone Home Assistant already tells apart.
  • Entities for your automations. Each device you name gets a device tracker (its state is the room, or not_home), an area sensor and distance sensors. Events fire when a named device arrives, leaves or changes room, and Assist can answer "Which room is Alex in?" once you add its sentences. See chapter 14.
  • Follow, for one device: its room now and its movements, with alerts by phone or email when it changes room, arrives or leaves.
  • Live Tracker, a full-screen live map, and Occupancy, an estimate of how many people are in the building.
  • Traceback: 7 days of where your devices went, played back on your floors, with Insights (time in each room) and New Objects (chapter 12).
  • Share a replay: a 10 to 30 second clip of people moving through the house, with names hidden, made in your browser. Nothing is uploaded.
  • Yesterday at home (off until you turn it on): one message each morning about the day before, with comings and goings, guests, the rooms empty longest and lights left on in empty rooms (chapter 13).
  • Lights follow people: a room's lights go on when someone with a named phone, watch or tag comes in, and off once the room has been empty for its delay. A light turned off by hand stays off until everyone has left (chapter 14). Three Home Assistant blueprints do the same in your own automations.
  • Guest tags: a small Bluetooth tag lent to a guest for their visit, with rooms they shouldn't go into and a report of the stay (chapter 7).
  • Calibration, including Walk a room: carry your phone round a room for about 90 seconds so PadSpan learns what it sounds like (chapter 11).
  • Positions between receivers. With three or more receivers placed on a plan with a scale, PadSpan also places devices between them, even in a room with no receiver of its own.

Your floor plan, in any form

These need only Home Assistant.

  • A rough house from your Home Assistant rooms: one box per area on a blank floor, from Show what it will make and Make the rough house (chapter 9).
  • Any plan you have: a picture or a photo of a sketch, a PDF, a blank floor of a size you type, a plan file from Easy Floorplan, a DXF or a Floorplanner file, a Sweet Home 3D file, a room scan from an iPhone or iPad Pro with LiDAR (drawn to scale, nothing to measure), or a robot vacuum's map (experimental). See chapter 9.
  • Rooms, floors and heights: draw each room, set each floor's height, and give a room its ceiling height and split level.
  • Walls and doors: wall materials and open edges for the signal, and doors and windows on the walls, linked to their sensors (chapter 10).
  • The Overview draws your floors stacked as a house. The house in 3D, Live Aboard, is PadSpan Pro's.

Also free

  • Enhanced security (on for a new install): only administrators change the shared setup, and everyone in the house can view and use it (chapter 21).
  • Backups and restore, and Home Assistant's own backup carries all of PadSpan (chapter 22).
  • Training, the animated walkthroughs, and the help inside PadSpan (the ? next to a heading).

What PadSpan Pro adds

PadSpan Pro is one key on the same install, with nothing to reinstall and nothing to set up again. It adds:

  • Every light and device exactly where it is, in its real shape, size and angle, with Automorph, Showcase, WLED's Advanced controls and moving rain and snow (chapter 15).
  • Live Aboard, your house in 3D, like looking into a house in The Sims: lit windows at night, rain and snow, the Studio sky, a walk through the house at eye height, and a view that flies to the doorbell, a door or an alarm. It is experimental (chapter 16).
  • Furnish: furniture, stairs, floors and wall colours, devices that come alive when the real one runs, and a figure for each person (chapter 17).
  • Wall mode on the Atlas page, and the Atlas's new layout, where each screen picks what it shows (chapter 18).
  • The Atlas as your Overview, and people and tags on the map.
  • The AI helpers that build the house from your floor plans (chapter 19).
  • Every add-on (below), and every new feature while the key runs.

One Pro key covers up to five homes (with more than two, each keeps its usage report on).

When a key runs out, it keeps working for 14 more days. After that, nothing you set up is deleted. Light placement goes read-only, Forensics stops recording (what it recorded stays for its retention time), and the 3D house, the wall screen, Locate and Busy Times switch off. It all comes back as you left it when you renew.

The free trial

With no key on the install, Settings → Features → PadSpan licence has Start 90-day free trial: all of Pro for 90 days, every add-on included, no card, one trial per home. An administrator starts it. It needs the usage report on, and the report stays on until the trial ends. Keys and prices are on padspan.traks.ca. The trial, keys, homes and testers are explained in chapter 25.

Just want one thing?

An add-on adds one feature to an install without Pro. One payment covers 2 years on up to three homes, and one key carries every add-on you buy. PadSpan Pro includes all of them. What each one adds: chapter 25, and in more detail PadSpan Pro and the add-ons. They're sold at padspan.traks.ca/#addons.

Is it for me?

PadSpan is a good fit if:

  • You want your house on one screen: the lights in their rooms a tap away, the locks, and the alarms. That needs only Home Assistant, with no receiver.
  • You want to know which room someone is in, not only whether they are home. That needs a receiver in each room you want told apart. One receiver tells home from away and nothing more.
  • You already have Bluetooth proxies, or Shellys (Gen2 or newer), or can put an ESP32 board in a few rooms. PadSpan uses what Home Assistant already hears. It needs no firmware of its own.
  • Everyone you want shown in a room carries a phone, a watch or a tag. An Android phone with the Home Assistant Companion app works after one switch in the app. An iPhone or Apple Watch needs its key (the IRK) once. A beacon or key tag with a fixed address works as soon as you name it.
  • You like to see it. PadSpan shows who is where on a map of your house, and lets you replay it.

Think again, or start small, if:

  • You need to know exactly where someone stands. Bluetooth gives a room, and with enough receivers a rough spot within it. Walls, bodies and furniture change the signal, so a device near a doorway can show in the room next door.
  • Your devices change their Bluetooth address and have no key you can get. An AirTag, a Samsung SmartTag or a phone without the Companion app or its IRK can be heard but not told apart for long. PadSpan has an experimental switch for AirTags (MAC Rotation Bridging), but it can't promise them.
  • Your Home Assistant runs on a 2 GB machine near a busy street. PadSpan hears every passing device. More than 2 GB of memory is recommended. See chapter 2.
  • You want it outdoors, over a large garden or a farm. Rooms come from receivers; outside, each area you want told apart needs its own.

You don't need a floor plan to start. Install it, give each receiver its area, and Who's where now shows rooms the same day. Chapter 4 walks through it.

What PadSpan never touches

PadSpan reads your other integrations and leaves them as they are. Installing, disabling or removing it doesn't change another integration's devices, entities or settings. The only exceptions are buttons you press yourself, one at a time: adding an iPhone's key to Private BLE Device, giving a receiver its area, switching on a phone's BLE Transmitter sensor, and Manage's delete buttons. The README has the full list, and what leaves the house is under Updates & Privacy.
