16. Live Aboard

Contents

Live Aboard is the Atlas in 3D: your floors, rooms, walls, doors, windows, lights and sensors as a small house you can turn, zoom and tap, the way you'd look into a house in The Sims. It is built from the map you already drew, so there is nothing new to set up before you see it. It comes with PadSpan Pro, and it is experimental: if something looks wrong in your house, please say so (chapter 27).

Turning it on

  1. Open Settings → Atlas and find Atlas — Mapped Light Control.
  2. Under Live Aboard, turn on Show Live Aboard on the Atlas. It saves at once.
  3. Open the Atlas. In the sidebar Atlas, tap 🏠 on the left rail to switch between the flat map and Live Aboard. In Mapping → Atlas builder, use the Map | Live Aboard switch beside the zoom.

The same card has:

  • Rain and snow: falls outside the house when the Atlas's weather says so.
  • Look: the Atlas's colours, Live Aboard's own, or Studio (its own colours on a stage under a sky that follows the sun, with soft light and shadows).
  • Back to home view: how long the sidebar waits untouched before it flies back to its home view (Off, 30 s, 1 min, 5 min).
  • Show people and Show tags & scanners, on the map and in Live Aboard.

Set it up step by step on that card (or Set up Live Aboard at the top of the Atlas builder) opens a wizard that builds the house with you: floor heights, rooms, devices, heights, doors and windows, LED strips, furniture, the extras, and making it your screen (Show the Atlas as Overview). Each step says what it found and what's left.

If 🏠 is greyed out, the reason shows beside it (a browser without WebGL, say). Tap it to try again.

Getting around

In the sidebar, Live Aboard opens on every floor, pulled apart (⇕ Explode), with the page's bars out of the way. ☰ (top right) brings the bars back.

Walls (bottom left): Cut lowers the walls in front of you; Up stands every wall at full height (best from outside, and at night); Down lays them all low; Glass makes the walls in front of you see-through, so rooms keep their height; Roof shows the roof from outside, or never.

Floors: ▲ / ▼ step a floor up or down and hide the floors above; All shows every floor; ⇕ Explode (with All) pulls them apart. On a keyboard, PgUp / PgDn change floor and Home shows them all. Tap a floor's name for its sheet.

Views ▾ has every floor, Whole house, and views you keep with Save this view. Set as home makes the current view the one the sidebar opens on and goes back to. Views ▾ → Look picks this screen's colours: Same as Settings, Classic, Studio, or a Showcase theme. Tick By the sun for one look by day and another after sunset.

Rooms ▾ lists every room under its floor. Pick one: the view flies to it and its panel opens, with the room's devices and buttons (each switch with Undo), its scenes, and More devices of the area: what Home Assistant has in that area that the map doesn't show. On a wide screen, Room chips along the top in ⋯ puts the room names in a row instead.

Walking through the house

Walk (bottom right; on a narrow screen, in View ▾) puts you in the house at eye height, just inside the front door, or in the room you have open. Every floor shows and every wall stands while you walk.

Walls, railings, window sills and closed doors stop you. A door with a sensor opens when the sensor says it is open; a door with no sensor stands as its sheet says (ajar inside, shut on an outside wall). Stairs take you up to the floor they reach and back down. Out through an open outside door, you are on the ground round the house. Furniture does not stop you. The stick shows on a touch screen, or as soon as you touch the view.

Stop walking puts the camera, the floors (pulled apart or not) and the walls back as they were. Standing still, Live Aboard draws nothing, as it does when you are not walking.

Reading the house

What you see depends on how close you are:

  • The whole house: a tag beside each floor with what's going on ("Main · 3 lights on · 1 open · 2 people"). Tap it to see that floor alone.
  • One floor: room names, readings and sensor markers.
  • One room (double-tap or zoom in): the device codes in that room; the others fade back.

Alarms (leak, smoke, gas, CO) always show: rings across the room's floor and a red word over everything (WET, SMOKE, GAS, CO, or ALARM while latched), until it is reset from the banner.

  • Lights glow in their own colour and brightness. A light that's off is a small dark fixture.
  • Room readings (temperature, humidity, air quality) show under the name. One that hasn't come in for over an hour shows a dash ("–°"), so you never read an old one as current.
  • Motion: a sensor tripping now is a bright blue badge and tints its room. One that tripped earlier is ringed in its age colour, from blue (just now) to red (about 2 hours). A dashed line drops from each badge to where the sensor really is. The Motion chip at the top names the newest rooms ("Kitchen now · Hall 12 min").
  • Heat map: ☰ → Temps colours each room with a thermometer: blue when cool, green around 21°, red from 26°.
  • Window open in the rain: an open window glows blue and a line under the Motion chip names it. It needs the window drawn with its sensor linked, and the Atlas's weather set up.
  • At night the house darkens with the real sun, and with the walls up the windows of lit rooms glow.
  • Doors and windows linked to sensors open and shut as they do. A lock linked to a door glows red while unlocked.

Mezzanines and galleries: make one a floor of its own in Home Assistant. In Mapping → 3D Stack → Floor Heights, give the hall under it its full height and the mezzanine its real base elevation; Live Aboard then draws it inside the hall. Mark an open edge in Mapping → Edit → Signal walls (Open edge: tap a room edge) and press Railing on its row for a railing (chapter 10).

Using it

  • Tap a light to switch it; hold it for brightness, colour and effects.
  • Tap a sensor or a reading for its card; a motion sensor's opens its activity calendar.
  • Tap a room's name for its sheet: every device, All lights on / off, fans, and the room's own scenes and scripts.

Building: doors, windows, heights and strips

Building happens in Mapping → Atlas builder, in Live Aboard, with Edit. Nothing is stored until you press Save. Undo, Redo and Discard step through your changes; Done leaves Edit and asks first if something isn't saved. Keys: Ctrl+Z undo, Ctrl+Y redo, Escape lets go, Delete removes the picked door or window.

Doors, windows and doorways

  1. Edit, then Door, Window or Doorway. The view looks straight down.
  2. Press on a wall and drag along it, or tap its two ends. The length shows, in 5 cm steps.
  3. Let go: the opening's sheet opens. A door has a height, a Type (hinged, double, sliding, barn, pocket, bifold, garage, gate and others), hinge and swing, and Follows: a door or garage sensor, or a cover, so it opens and shuts as that does. A window has Sill and Head heights.

Move or reshape a room in Mapping → Layout and its doors and windows go with it when you press Save corrections. Any that can't are left where they were, and the message says how many.

Heights: Edit → Heights, tap a device, and use the slider or a spot offered for its kind (Ceiling, Corner, High on the wall, Switch height, Table, Floor…). Reset to default puts it back; for a light, What is this? says what it is (a pot light, a pendant, a lamp). These are the same heights as the Atlas builder's Height row.

Strips (LED strips, valances, coves, string lights): Edit → Strip, tap the light or pick it from Lights to lay out, then Round this room (with Leave gaps at doors and windows), Under, Behind or Top edge of a piece of furniture, Along a rail, or Draw. Set its height (Toe-kick, Counter, Under cabinets, Valance, Cove, Ceiling edge, or centimetres) and which way it shines. Remove run puts it back to PadSpan's guess.

Furniture: Mapping → Furnish, chapter 17.

Building from your plans with an AI (experimental)

Three tabs under Edit → Plans read your floor plans with an AI. Each is an experiment: the AI gets things wrong, so check every item. They need PadSpan Pro and switches in Settings → AI (chapter 19). A cloud AI gets a copy of the plans it reads.

  • Plans (needs AI map-building research (experimental) and an AI Task): the AI marks stairs, and if you tick Lights, light symbols, as numbered pins over your plan. Go through them one at a time (New, Correction, Failure, Looks right, Later); there is no accept-all. On a small local AI about 2 in 10 stair suggestions are wrong. It never places or moves a light, and only ever writes your 3D house.
  • Line up plans (needs Build from plans and Claude Opus 5.5 or newer through Home Assistant's Anthropic integration): lines up several plans of one floor (a measured one, an architect's sheet, a scan). Only a plan's picture moves; calibration points, rooms, receivers and the plan that set the scale stay where they are. Each save takes a safety backup first.
  • Rooms from plans (same switch): Claude reads each plan twice and proposes outlines for the rooms you already have, matched by where they lie, never by name. Rooms both reads agree on are ticked; Look closely shows where they differ, and Read again makes a third read. Accept all for this floor saves after one backup; Undo this rooms session takes it back.

On a wall screen

  • Set as home the view the screen should rest on, and pick Back to home view. It flies home after that long untouched, never while you are editing or have a card open.
  • The corner button makes it full screen.
  • With nothing changing, Live Aboard draws nothing, so a quiet house costs the screen nothing.

More in chapter 18.

When something's off

  • It shows the flat map instead: the browser couldn't run the 3D view (no WebGL, too slow, or its graphics restarted). The 🏠 button says why; tap it to try again. More in chapter 26.
  • A room's temperature shows "–°": its sensor hasn't reported for over an hour.
  • A device floats in the wrong place: set its height in Edit → Heights, or move it on the map.
  • A door or window is missing: draw it in Edit, or on the map with its sensor linked.
