12. The screens

Contents

What each of PadSpan's screens is for, where it is in the menu, and the everyday ones in more detail: Overview, Live Tracker, Follow, Occupancy, Traceback, Forensics and Health. Most screens and cards have their own ? help beside the heading, in English, German, French and Dutch.

Finding your way

  • The menu is down the left side. On a phone it is a bar along the bottom, and More opens the whole menu.
  • The mode button, top right (on a phone, in the bar at the top), reads Basic, Advanced or Dev. Each press moves to the next mode. This browser remembers your choice.
  • The Data button, beside it, shows Live (your house) or Sample (the sample house). See The sample house.
  • The status dot, in the top bar, shows only when something needs a look. See Health.

Basic, Advanced and Development

Every screen is in the menu in every mode. The mode decides two things: which screens are in the main list (the rest are under More tools at the bottom of the menu, closed until you open it), and how much detail each screen shows.

  • Basic: a short main list, and each screen keeps to the essentials. For anyone at home who just wants to see where things are. A link to part of an expert screen (a room's outline, a receiver's place, the floor heights) opens just that part, with a way back, and the mode stays Basic.
  • Advanced: the default. A longer main list, and every tool on each screen.
  • Development: the same menu as Advanced, with more diagnostic detail inside the screens. Use it when troubleshooting, or when support asks.

Changing the mode deletes nothing. In Advanced and Development, Settings → UI Structure can move Devices, Bluetooth, Presence, Monitor, QA or Sandbox from More tools into the main list.

The sample house

The sample house is a made-up house on three floors with every feature set up, so you can see what PadSpan does before your own setup is finished. It is read-only: anything that would change it is refused with a message, and nothing you do there touches your own house. Only how the panel looks (its theme and style) can be changed there.

  • A new install starts on the sample house and switches to your own by itself once PadSpan hears your first receiver.
  • Preview a finished house, on the Overview's Who's where now card, opens it.
  • The Data button: from Live, the first press turns it into Show demo data?; press again within 3 seconds to open the sample house. From Sample, one press goes back to Live.
  • Back to my house, on the yellow banner, goes back to your own.

Live or Sample is this browser's own choice: someone previewing the sample house doesn't switch a wall screen or anyone else's browser. If the house itself is still set to Sample and Enhanced security is on, only an administrator can switch it back to Live.

Overview

The first page: where everyone and everything is now. In Live mode it updates every 5 seconds.

What you see depends on how far setup has got:

  • No room drawn yet: Who's where now, each room and who is in it, from your receivers' Home Assistant areas (chapter 4), and a Make it a map (optional) card with Setup's steps.
  • Rooms on the map: the map, a 3D stack of your floors. Each room has its own colour. Receivers are rings with a short name, green when reporting and grey when offline. Devices you follow are amber markers with their name and how sure PadSpan is of the spot ("Room only" when it knows the room but not the spot); they dim and say "(Away)" when away. Turn on Pins to show your other named devices too.

Basic keeps it short: the Quiet tick, counts of rooms, objects, receivers and calibration points, the Track Your Phone card, and the map under Your home.

Advanced and Development add People in building (click it to see how the number was reached) and, under the map, cards for Rooms, Objects, Bluetooth radios and Calibration. Their buttons open full lists: View rooms list, All objects and Unidentified, View radios list. Click a row for its details. Clear unidentified removes devices nobody has named or followed; they come back if heard again.

Quiet, top right, hides devices nobody has named or followed, here and on the other screens. It changes what is shown, not what is tracked.

Other cards come and go: Getting started while setup isn't done, Improve accuracy with a last tip or two once it is, the Pro trial, what changed after an update, and Positioning Diagnostics (folded) for support. With PadSpan Pro, Settings → Atlas → Show the Atlas as Overview puts the Atlas here instead.

Live Tracker

The live map on its own, as large as the screen allows: good for a wall tablet or a second screen.

  • Drag to move the map; the mouse wheel or a pinch zooms; +, − and ⌂ in the corner do the same, and ⌂ resets the view. Search objects, rooms, scanners… finds one and moves the map to it.
  • Top left: counts of rooms, named devices (Tracked) and receivers (Radios). Top right: your receivers, each with how many devices it hears; red means it isn't scanning.
  • When a device changes room, it shows briefly on the map and in a short list of recent moves.
  • Below the map, Tracked Devices lists the devices you follow and their rooms; the target icon centres the map on one. ℹ hides or shows these panels.
  • Controls opens the drawing controls (the floor, the gap between floors, walls, trails, pins, heat), as on the Overview's map.

For a wall screen, open it with ?view=purelive&kiosk=1 on the end of the panel's address: the panel's menus are hidden.

Follow

Where one person or thing is right now: a phone, a key fob, a tag, anything Bluetooth PadSpan hears.

  1. Pick it under Choose a tag to follow: click the search box, or type part of a name, room or address. The list is sorted by signal, strongest first. Picking one also adds it to your followed devices, so the Overview draws it as an amber marker.
  2. The card below says which room it is in now, its floor, and when a receiver last heard it. A Calibrated badge with a percentage shows when its place comes from your calibration points.
  3. The Location map draws every room as a box, the tag a pulsing dot in its room. It is a room diagram, not your floor plan.

Name this tag (or Rename) gives it a name. Details → opens everything PadSpan knows about it. In Advanced and Development, the card also lists the receivers that hear it, and a Movement log card its recent room changes.

Under the card:

  • 📍 Locate (PadSpan Pro or the Presence insights add-on): which way to walk to find it. Under You are carrying:, pick your own phone. In the same room, it shows the distance in metres, getting closer or further. In another room, it says which room to head to next, and up or down a floor. No compass, no camera.
  • Movement Alerts: tell you when it changes room, arrives or leaves, on your phone or by email. See chapter 13.

Occupancy

How many people are in the building, never how many devices.

  • Known people come from your Home Assistant person entities.
  • Unknown people come from phones nobody has claimed, and from rooms whose occupancy or motion sensors say someone is there when no known person is.
  • Things (keys, tags) are listed under Seen, not people, and never counted.

At the top: the count, a range when it isn't sure, how confident it is, and the counts behind it. Counted lists each person and how they were found; Rooms with evidence and How the number was reached show the working.

  • Hybrid counting also uses person entities and occupancy and motion sensors; untick it to count phones only.
  • Phone clustering: phones heard at nearly the same strength by every receiver count as one person (a phone and its watch). Save & Refresh applies a new threshold.
  • Record the real count: type how many people are really there and Save. The Accuracy card then shows how often PadSpan's estimate and the real count agree.

The same number is the People in building card on the Overview.

Traceback

A replay of where your devices went. About every 10 seconds PadSpan records where each device you have named or follow is; unnamed devices aren't recorded. It keeps 7 days.

The row at the top switches between four modes:

  • Playback: pick the Time range (5 minutes to 7 days, or a custom range) and All objects or one device. ▶ plays the whole period; Run time (1 to 60 minutes) is how long the replay takes. Step a frame, jump to the start or end, or drag the timeline. Each device is drawn where live tracking placed it at that moment, or at the centre of its room when there was no position. Export CSV and Export JSON save the frames.
    • Share a replay turns the chosen stretch into a 10 to 30 second clip of people moving through the house, ready to post. Names become Person 1, Person 2 (or initials); device names, addresses and signal readings are left out; room names stay as they are or become generic ones (Bedroom, Room 3); floors are numbered, never named, and only the time of day shows, not the date. The clip is drawn from your rooms' outlines, so no plan image, address or coordinates are in it, and it starts where the first person changes room. The dialog plays the clip and lists what it hides (and each name and room as it will show) before you press Save clip. Pick Wide or Vertical, and MP4 (best for Reddit and X; Chrome and Edge make it), WebM (Firefox) or GIF. A small made with PadSpan mark sits in a corner; untick it to leave it out. The clip is made in your browser and saved to your computer: nothing is uploaded. A video records in real time, so keep the tab in front until it is saved. It plays on the floor stack; the 3D house comes later. Free in every edition.
    • Full house activity (PadSpan Pro or the Time travel add-on) plays the same back on your Atlas, and with 💡 Devices on, the house too: lights, locks, motion, doors and windows, and sensor readings, from Home Assistant's recorder.
  • New Objects: which devices were first heard in a window (up to 7 days back).
  • Insights: two tables for the last 1, 3 or 7 days. Time in Room: how long each device spent in each room each day, and how many times it went in. Peak Concurrent Occupancy: the most tracked devices in one room at once, each day, and when.
  • Busy Times (PadSpan Pro or the Presence insights add-on): one box per room, busiest first, each with a 24-hour bar chart of when it gets busy.

Insights and Busy Times work without a floor plan. Manage → History → Open History has the saved list of room changes, each with ▶ Traceback to play that moment back.

Forensics

Which Bluetooth devices were near your receivers between two times: after a break-in or a package theft, or to spot an unknown tracker that keeps coming back. It needs PadSpan Pro or the Forensics add-on, and is off until you turn it on.

Turn it on: Settings → Features → Forensics → Enable presence-session recording. It asks you to confirm. While it is on, PadSpan checks every 60 seconds which devices it hears and records presence sessions: stretches of time a device was heard. More than 5 minutes of silence ends a session. Forensics is then in the main list in every mode.

Search: pick a start and an end (local times), or Last hour, Last 24 h or Last 7 days, then Search window.

  • Recorded — actually present: the devices with a session in the window, the longest stay first, with their time in the window (Dwell), strongest signal and the receivers that heard them.
  • Hide my labelled devices hides the ones you named, so strangers stand out.
  • Possible appears only when the window reaches back before recording began.
  • Export CSV saves the results.

A Bluetooth address is not a person. Phones change their address, so most phones show as one-off addresses; devices with a fixed address (earbuds, trackers, speakers) are what it finds reliably. Addresses can be faked. Treat results as leads, not proof.

Privacy: it records every device nearby, neighbours' and passers-by's included. Recordings stay in your Home Assistant and are never uploaded. The Settings card keeps them for 7, 14 (the default), 30, 60 or 90 days, even after you turn recording off; Delete all recorded data erases them.

Health

Is PadSpan working? Health gathers PadSpan's own checks in one page. Clicking the status dot opens it in any mode.

The status dot shows only when something is wrong, with how many issues; hover over it for their names.

  • Red: PadSpan isn't answering, so live data has stopped; positioning's last update failed; or no receiver is reporting.
  • Amber: a receiver hasn't been heard for over 10 minutes; a receiver hears far fewer devices than usual; or learning is paused, so rooms come from live signals alone.

It waits a few seconds before it shows, so one slow moment doesn't make it blink. Receivers you marked lost, disabled or excluded don't count, and it watches only live data. On a wall screen only red shows.

On the page:

  • System: the version, Live or Sample, and how long the last refresh took. Open Diagnostics is here for support (chapter 27).
  • Hearing less than usual: a receiver hearing far fewer Bluetooth messages than normal for over 10 minutes while the others are fine. Rooms near it can be wrong; restarting it often helps.
  • Scanner Health: for each receiver, how often it agrees with the room the others point to (Agreement) and the same as a figure from 0.5 to 1 (Reliability). The figure is shown only: a low one doesn't make that receiver count for less.
  • Room and receiver layout: PadSpan's model of your home in metres, a list of checks that pass or fail, and its tools (Resync Scanners, Run Radio Audit, Reset Spatial Model and more).
  • Problems found: automatic checks of the whole setup, most serious first, each marked Fix this, Worth fixing or Good to know, with an Action: line and a button to the screen where you fix it. Under it, Room mix-ups lists the room pairs PadSpan keeps moving a device between, and Map Calibration Quality each plan's calibration error.

The other screens

  • Setup: chapter 9. Mapping: chapter 9; the Atlas builder is in the Atlas guide.
  • Calibration: chapter 11.
  • Rooms: each room's colour, outline (with Edit outline, or Put it on the map for a room drawn only on a plan), ceiling height, split level, receivers and who is in it now. Rooms and floors are your Home Assistant areas and floors, so their names change in Home Assistant.
  • Receivers: every receiver with its room, floor, height, signal adjustment and status. Height and signal adjustment change right in the table. Problems only shows just the ones that need attention.
  • Settings: every setting is in chapter 20. In Basic it has no tabs.
  • Manage: Data (clean-ups, deletions, Backup & Restore), HA Entities, Beacon Characteristics, History, Events (alerts by email or phone), Logs and Factory Reset. See chapter 22 and chapter 13.
  • Training: animated walkthroughs and the Full Manual inside the panel. Some of it is still in English only.
  • Guests: the Guest tags guide and chapter 13.
  • Devices: All Devices, By Room (every device with its room and signal, where you name devices), Registry (PadSpan's fixed ID for each device, to merge duplicates) and Door Openers.
  • Bluetooth: who hears what (Visualisation), Advertisements, Scanners (each receiver's signal adjustment, exclude, minimum signal), IRK Manager (phones' and watches' keys) and ESPHome Configs (ready-made settings for building a receiver).
  • Monitor: what PadSpan hears, in numbers, by receiver and by room, and Export for support.
  • QA: checks on your setup, backups, and each receiver ranked.
  • Presence, Sandbox: for experts; they change nothing.
  • Install Base: the developer's screen for the opt-in usage reports. It asks for a PIN and shows nothing about other people's homes.
