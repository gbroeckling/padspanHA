Appendix C. Accuracy benchmark

Contents

How often does PadSpan get the room right in your house, measured on walks it has never seen? tools/benchmark/ answers that from a recorded walk, with a plain "nearest receiver" answer beside it. It is a tool for developers and power users: it isn't shipped in the integration and changes nothing in an install. For the everyday score, use Calibration → Model instead (chapter 11).

Why held out, and grouped

Most room-presence scores test against the same walk that was just used to calibrate. Neighbouring readings share most of their radio conditions, so a reading left out is answered by its near-copies and the score reads close to perfect. Published work shows the size of the gap: k-NN had a 0.0 m median on a random split and 9.4 m when whole positions were held out (arXiv 2610.22680).

So the benchmark never splits readings at random. A reading is tested only against fingerprints from other groups:

--group-byHeld out togetherUse when
walk (default with 2+ walks)A whole walkYou recorded the house more than once, ideally on different days
visit (default with 1 walk)One stay in a roomYou have one walk
positionA visit, plus every reading within 1.5 m of its marked spots (--radius)The walk has x/y marks
--train-walk NAMEEverything except NAMEA calibration walk and separate test walks

--settle 5 drops the first 5 seconds of every visit (you were still in the doorway when you marked the room).

Recording a walk in PadSpan

  1. Turn on Settings → Features → RSSI Vector Capture (off by default).
  2. In Health, under RSSI Vector Capture, choose ⏺ Record and give it a label: the label becomes the walk's name.
  3. Carry the phone or tag. Each time you arrive in a room, pick it and choose Mark room. Typing the spot's x/y as well (metres, the map's own) gives metre errors and --group-by position.
  4. Stay 1 to 2 minutes in each room, moving around. ⏹ Stop, then Export .jsonl.
  5. Repeat on another day for a second walk.
python tools/benchmark/from_capture.py padspan-capture-*.jsonl -o walk.json

Only readings under a mark are kept; the receivers' rooms and positions, the room centres and what PadSpan showed at the time come along. --device KEY keeps one device when several were marked.

A capture holds your receiver and device addresses and room names. Keep it private unless you mean to share it.

A walk from another system

A walk log is JSON:

{
  "format": "padspan-walk", "version": 1,
  "scanners": {"kitchen_proxy": {"room": "Kitchen", "x": 2.0, "y": 3.5, "floor": "main", "z": 2.1}},
  "rooms":    {"Kitchen": {"floor": "main", "polygon": [[0,0],[4,0],[4,4],[0,4]]}},
  "floors":   {"main": 0.0},
  "polls": [
    {"ts": 1761300000.0, "walk": "sat-am", "device": "phone", "room": "Kitchen",
     "x": 1.2, "y": 2.0, "floor": "main",
     "rssi": {"kitchen_proxy": -61.0, "hall_proxy": -78.5}}
  ]
}

Each reading needs room (the true room) and rssi (dBm for each receiver that heard it). Optional: ts (needed for --settle), walk, device, x/y/floor, and live (what the system under test said). A receiver's room feeds the baseline; its x/y are needed for the spatial method. CSV works too: ts,walk,device,room,x,y,floor plus one rssi:<scanner> column per receiver, with the receivers in a second CSV given by --scanners.

Running it

pip install voluptuous
python tools/benchmark/benchmark.py walk.json [more.json ...] [--group-by ...] [--json results.json]

Home Assistant needn't be installed: PadSpan's modules are loaded straight from custom_components/padspan_ha. What is scored, all with PadSpan's own code:

  • PadSpan k-NN: the calibration match over the held-in readings.
  • PadSpan spatial: the path-loss solve, with each receiver's fit redone from the held-in readings. Needs at least 3 placed receivers.
  • Nearest scanner: the room of the receiver that hears the device loudest.
  • PadSpan live (as recorded): from a capture only. Not held out, so it is "what you saw", not a fair split.

Reading the results: rooms right is the share of readings where the room was right (one a method couldn't answer counts as wrong; answered shows how many it answered). median m and p75 m are metre errors over readings with a marked x/y. The JSON adds per-room shares and the most common wrong calls (true room → called room), which usually name the open-plan pair or the stairs.

Scoring any Home Assistant room sensor

python tools/benchmark/score_sensor.py walk.json history.json --entity sensor.phone_area [--map "living_room=Living Room"]

history.json is Home Assistant's history (its REST history, or the History panel's Download data CSV). At each reading, the sensor's last state at or before that moment is its answer; unknown, unavailable and not_home count as no answer. This works for Bermuda, PadSpan's own sensors and others. Running two systems side by side on the same walk is the fair comparison; please publish only comparisons where each was set up its own documented way.

A public dataset

Zenodo record 4783076 (CC BY 4.0): a house with 6 fixed beacons and 10 rooms, one calibration session (a laptop, 600 fingerprints) and two later test sessions (a laptop and a Raspberry Pi, 1,200 each).

python tools/benchmark/datasets/zenodo_4783076.py DATA_DIR -o house.json
python tools/benchmark/benchmark.py house.json --train-walk calibration-laptop

Result (PadSpan 0.38.165's code, 2026-10-25), trained on the calibration session only:

MethodRooms rightTest laptopTest Raspberry Pi
PadSpan k-NN84.2%88.4%79.9%
Nearest beacon52.7%53.6%51.8%

What that does and doesn't show:

  • The radios are the other way round from a PadSpan house (fixed beacons, a moving receiver).
  • The dataset doesn't say where the beacons are, so the spatial method can't run.
  • The rows are averaged fingerprints with no times: k-NN without PadSpan's smoothing over time, on cleaner input than one reading.
  • The Raspberry Pi row is a different receiver from the calibration one.

Limits, plainly

  • Each reading is scored on its own. Live PadSpan smooths rooms over time, so it usually does better on room changes and worse right after one; the live row from a capture shows the smoothed answer.
  • A walk is one person, one device, one afternoon. Several walks on different days, with doors in different states, are what make the number mean something.
  • The room you mark is only as true as your marking; --settle helps.
  • The baseline is deliberately simple. Beating it is a floor, not a boast.
