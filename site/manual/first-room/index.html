4. Your first room in 15 minutes

Contents

From a fresh install to "Alex's phone is in the Kitchen", with no floor plan. Then, if you want it, your house on a map. Everything on this page is free.

Before you start you need Home Assistant 2024.8 or newer, at least one Bluetooth receiver, and an area in Home Assistant for each room (Settings → Areas, labels & zones). See chapter 2.

1. Install PadSpan

On Home Assistant 2026.11 or newer:

  1. Open Settings → Marketplace and search for PadSpan.
  2. Open PadSpan HA and choose Install.
  3. The setup dialog opens straight away, with nothing to fill in. Choose Submit.
  4. Open PadSpan HA in the sidebar.

On older versions, install it with HACS and restart Home Assistant first. Both ways are in chapter 3. The other new sidebar entry, Atlas, is the map of your lights; leave it for later.

The sample house

Until PadSpan hears its first receiver, it shows a sample house with every feature set up, and a yellow banner says so. A new install switches to your own house by itself as soon as it hears a receiver. If you are looking at the sample house and want your own, choose Back to my house.

2. Give every receiver its room

PadSpan puts each device in the room whose receiver hears it best, and a receiver's room is the Home Assistant area of its device. A receiver with no area gives no room, however well it hears you: a phone right next to it lands in the next receiver's room. So this comes first.

The quickest place is the Overview's Who's where now card. Each receiver with no area has its own row there, with a list of your rooms:

  1. Check the room in the list. When the receiver's name points to a room ("esp32-kitchen"), that room is already chosen, and the row says Chosen from its name; change it if it's wrong.
  2. Choose Set room. The row says Room set: Kitchen.

This gives the receiver's device that area in Home Assistant, the same as setting it there. You can also:

  • set the area on the device in Home Assistant: Settings → Devices & services → Devices, open the receiver, edit its area;
  • in PadSpan, on the Overview's Bluetooth radios card (Advanced mode), choose View radios list, then Assign beside the receiver (or Change, if it has an area already).

A Shelly's Bluetooth scanner is a device of its own in Home Assistant: give that device the area. Shelly as a Bluetooth proxy shows how.

If Home Assistant has no areas yet, the card says so: add them first, in Settings → Areas, labels & zones.

3. See who's where

You don't need a floor plan for this. On the Overview, Who's where now lists each room your receivers cover, the named devices in it, and a count of the other Bluetooth devices heard there. It keeps itself up to date.

The line at the top says where you stand:

What it saysWhat it means
"No Bluetooth receivers found yet. Add an ESPHome Bluetooth proxy …"PadSpan hears no receiver. Add one, or use your Home Assistant machine's own Bluetooth. It shows up within a minute.
"… has no Home Assistant area yet, so PadSpan can tell home from away but not rooms."No receiver has an area. Give each one the area it's in (step 2).
"… cover one room, so you get home or away."All your receivers are in one area. Put one in another room.
"One receiver has no Home Assistant area yet, so it gives no room: …"The others work. Give this one its area.
"Your 3 receivers cover 3 rooms. …"It's working. Each device goes to the room whose receiver hears it best.

A row called Heard, no room yet holds named devices that only a receiver with no area can hear. Give that receiver its area.

4. Name your phone

A device you haven't named gets no entities from PadSpan. Naming one gives it a device tracker whose state is the room, an area sensor and distance sensors, and PadSpan follows it.

Android phone

  1. In the Home Assistant Companion app, open Settings → Companion app → Manage sensors → BLE Transmitter and switch it on. In the same place, set Transmit power to High and Advertise mode to Low latency: out of the box it sends too weakly to be heard well.
  2. In PadSpan's Overview, open the Track Your Phone card and choose Track this phone beside it (in Basic mode the button says Track).

PadSpan names the phone, follows it, and asks the app to start sending. If the button says Setup, the sensor isn't on in the app yet; if it says Enable & Track, it is switched off in Home Assistant, and that button switches it on (restart Home Assistant afterwards). In Advanced mode it then says how it went: Tracked!, Registered — waiting for signal (walk near a receiver), or Registered — enable BLE.

iPhone or Apple Watch

An iPhone changes its Bluetooth address every few minutes, so PadSpan needs its key, the IRK, once.

  1. Get the key and add it to Home Assistant's Private BLE Device integration. The steps, with an irk-capture ESP32 or a Mac, are in Which room is my phone in? and in PadSpan's own phone wizard (below).
  2. Once Home Assistant has it, the phone shows in Who's where now with a Track button beside its name. Choose Track. It can take a few minutes to show the first time.

With Enhanced security on, only an administrator sees Track.

A beacon or key tag

On Follow, pick it under Choose a tag to follow and choose Name this tag. Or, on the Overview's Objects card (Advanced mode), open All objects, choose Tag on its row, type a name and choose Save label. Near a busy street that list is long: change All statuses to Identified, or search by name. Which Bluetooth tag works? has what to buy.

Once a phone or tag has a name, it is in Who's where now by name, and your automations can use its room: chapter 14.

That's your first room. The rest of this page is optional.

5. Make it a map

A map turns the list of rooms into your house, drawn on the Overview. Calibration needs it. With three or more receivers placed on it, PadSpan can also place devices between them, even in a room with no receiver of its own.

Choose Make it a map on the Who's where now card. It opens Setup, the steps in order:

  1. Upload a floor plan: any picture of the floor, even a photo of a sketch. No picture? No floor plan? Ways to get one offers a phone's room scan, your own sketch or a blank floor.
  2. Set the scale: click both ends of a wall you know, type its length and choose Save the scale. Don't skip it: nothing goes on the map without a scale.
  3. Draw the rooms: press a room's name above the plan, click each corner, and double-click the last one. Each room is saved as you finish it.
  4. Floors and their heights: only in a house with two indoor floors or more.
  5. Place receivers and set their heights: in Live BLE Receivers beside the plan, choose Add beside each receiver and drag it to where it is mounted. Or choose Put my receivers in their rooms: each goes in the middle of its room, to drag into place later.
  6. Track your phone or watch: done already if you named your phone in step 4 above.
  7. Calibrate: walk the house with your phone, so PadSpan learns what each spot sounds like.

Each step's button opens the tool for it, with a bar at the top that says which step you are on and ← Back to Setup. Skip for now moves on; Setup remembers where you were.

One step is easy to miss. The floor plan steps run in Mapping's Setup Wizard, which ends on Finish. Until you choose Put it on the map there, your rooms and receivers are only on the picture. Once the first room is on the map, your house takes the place of Who's where now on the Overview, so draw every room your receivers cover: a device in a room you haven't drawn may not show.

Every step is explained in chapter 9.

6. Calibrate

The strongest receiver is a good first answer. Calibration makes it better: it records what every receiver hears at known spots in your house. It needs a floor plan with a scale.

Setup's Calibrate step opens one of two ways:

  • Walk a room, where it is switched on: walk each room for 90 seconds with your phone. It is off on a new install. Turn it on in Settings → Tracking → Positioning and calibration → Walk a Room (Calibration); Setup has an Open that setting button.
  • Guided calibration, point by point: choose your phone and the floor, stand at a spot, mark it on the plan and let PadSpan listen. Aim for 10 to 20 spots on each floor, spread out.

The step counts as done at five calibration points. Everything about calibration is in chapter 11.

If it doesn't work

  • "Bluetooth feed unavailable": restart Home Assistant fully, Settings → System → Restart. After a first install a reload isn't enough.
  • Uploading a plan, saving the scale or starting the trial is refused: those need a Home Assistant administrator.
  • Mapping has only Library and Upload, or Settings has no tabs: the mode button at the top right has the panel in Basic. Every screen is still in the menu, the ones Basic leaves out under More tools. Choose the mode button again for Advanced.
  • A phone jumps between rooms: see chapter 26 and Why does my phone jump between rooms?
  • Something else: chapter 27.
