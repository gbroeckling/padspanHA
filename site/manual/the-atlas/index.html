15. The Atlas

Contents

The Atlas is PadSpan's device map: your floors and rooms drawn as a plan, with lights, fans, locks, sensors, doors and windows where they really are. A tap switches a light or a fan; a sensor says what it reads, and a lock or a linked door opens its card. It was called "Lights" until it grew past lighting; the filter chip for light fixtures is still called Lights.

Free and Pro

  • Free: floors, rooms, and each light and device placed where it really is, as a plain marker. Tap a marker, or its row, to switch it. Until December 1, 2026, placement is Pro's, and the free map shows one marker per light in its room.
  • PadSpan Pro: shapes, sizes and angles, Fit room, Automorph, Showcase, WLED's Advanced controls, Whole house presets, Vacation Mode, and openers built from relays.

Settings → Features → PadSpan licence has a one-time 90-day free trial (chapter 25). PadSpan Bright is the same Atlas on its own, without presence tracking, with its own Bright Pro key. A Bright Pro key unlocks the placement tools: placement, shapes and sizes, WLED's Advanced controls, Automorph and Showcase. Whole house presets, Vacation Mode and the heatmap need PadSpan Pro or their add-on.

Without Pro, an add-on unlocks one part of the Atlas (chapter 25): Audio; Alarm & security (Arm and Disarm in the emergency button, the emergency lights test, and a door's card with its opener and lock); Climate map (the heatmap); Vacation Mode & presets; the Looks pack (Showcase with its themes, the West Coast Forest and the presets bar, on the free map: shapes stay Pro's); and, from December 1, 2026, Robot vacuum (the robot on the map and in 3D, with its card).

Getting there

  • Mapping → Atlas builder: where you place and edit devices.
  • The Atlas page in Home Assistant's sidebar: a lighter view of the same map, without the rest of PadSpan. It is on by default; Settings → Atlas → Show the Atlas in the Home Assistant sidebar turns it off.
  • On a wall screen: chapter 18.

The map needs your floor plans and rooms first: chapter 9.

Placing devices

  1. In Mapping → Atlas builder, find the device in the row list. Queue all unplaced lines up several at once.
  2. Drag its pulsing pin onto the floor, or choose + Place and tap the map where it belongs.
  3. With PadSpan Pro, set its shape, size and angle in the inspector: a light can be a pendant, sconce, strip or chandelier, in real centimetres. Without Pro it is drawn as a plain marker, and a shape it already has is kept for when a key comes back.

Lights, fans, locks, and motion, temperature, humidity, air-quality and leak sensors are all placed the same way.

  • A temperature marker is tinted by its reading: blue under 20°, red from 20°, bright orange over 34°. A humidity reading shows as a percentage.
  • Motion looks the same whatever the sensor: a short flash, then a fade the longer it has been quiet. Motion stays quiet for a few minutes after Home Assistant restarts.
  • Outdoor gear (anything whose area is on an Outside floor: a shed, the garden, the drive) has no floor of its own on the flat map. Place it on a floor plate, outside the room it sits beside.

Heights. Every placed device has a height above its floor, which the 3D house uses. Pick a device and use its Height row: one of the spots offered for its kind (a motion sensor: Corner, Ceiling, High on the wall; a leak sensor: Floor), centimetres, or Default. The Heights list shows every placed device; tick several to set them at once.

Using the map

  • A tap switches a light or a fan. On a sensor, a tap says what it reads; on a lock, it opens the lock's card. A hold (a gold ring fills as you hold), or Shift or Alt with a click, selects a device for editing. A drag moves it.
  • Devices on top of each other: hover, or hold a touch, and the box at the top left names everything stacked there. Alt+click steps through them.
  • A room's sheet: tap the room's name for every device in it, All lights on / off, its fans, and a button for each of the room's scenes and scripts in Home Assistant (up to six). Tap a floor's badge for the floor's sheet.
  • Find active scrolls to the first device doing something now (a light on, motion) and points it out.
  • On a touchscreen, pinch zooms the map, and a pan survives the map's refresh.

Doors, windows and locks

A door or window marked on a wall (chapter 10) shows open or shut from its sensor. Invert, next to Delete in the wall list, flips a sensor that reports backwards: if a door shows the wrong state, check that first. A lock on the map shows its state. A tap doesn't turn it: a tap or a hold opens its card, with Lock or Unlock, and Unlock asks first.

A door or window can also carry what opens it and what locks it (PadSpan Pro or the Alarm & security add-on; a Bright Pro key too). Tick a cover (a garage door, a gate, a blind), switch, button or script on Devices → Door Openers, then link it to the door or window in Mapping → Atlas builder, beside its sensor and its lock. A tap on the door then opens one card: whether it is open or closed, Open or Close for a cover (Trigger for a switch, button or script), and the lock. A cover linked with no sensor says open or shut itself.

Alarms and the emergency banner

Leak, smoke, gas and carbon monoxide sensors are drawn as a puddle or a round detector, and say WET, SMOKE, GAS or CO, ALARM while latched, and DRY or CLEAR when quiet. When one goes off, a red ring sweeps through its room, and it stays alarming for 2 days or until someone resets it, even if the sensor goes quiet. A banner shows it on every PadSpan screen, with its own Reset. Resetting clears PadSpan's memory of the alarm, not the sensor. More in chapter 13.

The scene row

Under the map, and in Live Aboard, is a row of tiles: one for each Home Assistant scene that sets a placed light, and one for each Whole house preset whose lit lights are all on one floor (marked Preset). A tile is a small map of the floor, showing which lights the scene uses. A tap runs it; when all its lights are already on, a tap turns them off. + New scene and ✎ open Home Assistant's own scene editor, for an administrator, and never on a wall screen.

Audio

Tick Audio in the map's ⋯ (Show on this screen, in the rail beside the map, and in the 3D view's own ⋯) and each room's sheet (in 3D, its panel) gains its music, shades and thermostat. It is off until a screen ticks it, and each screen (a phone, a wall) has its own tick. Off, the map and the sheets are exactly as before. Set up › on the same line opens the matching card (below) right there over the map, ticked or not, for an administrator, on a wall screen too.

  • On the map: a small speaker beside a room's name while one of its players is on, struck through while the room's first player is muted. Nothing while they are all off, and no other new marks. Tap it, or the name, for the room's sheet: a tap on the map never changes anything. The 3D view shows the same speaker at the room's name (where the names are hidden, where the name would be); a tap on it opens the room's panel.

  • The sheet: under the room's name, its temperature and humidity (the middle reading of its sensors) and who is there. Room off (n) turns off the room's lights and its players, never a lock, a shade, the thermostat or a plug, and shows only while something is on. Then the scenes, as before, and:

    • Audio: a row for each player, named by its system ("Control4 · Great Room", "Denon · Zone 2"), with whether it is on, off or muted, Mute, −, the level, + and ⏻ (that player off), these five always on one line. What a player can't do is left out or greyed: a player that is off shows its level as —.
    • the room's lights, as before;
    • Shades: one row for all the room's covers, Open, Stop and Close for all of them at once, and how far open they are. Garage doors, gates and doors stay on their door's card.
    • Climate: each thermostat's temperature now, its setpoint with − and +, and its mode.

    Each press is one call to Home Assistant, with no question first. Anyone who can switch the lights can use them, on a wall screen too. If Home Assistant refuses, it says why in its own words.

  • Matching the players to the rooms: Home Assistant doesn't know which room a whole-house system's zone is in, so an administrator matches them once. Match rooms at the end of the Audio part (Match Control4 to HA where Control4 is the only system) opens a card with every player, by system, and a room for each, or Not on the map. The first time, it guesses from the player's Home Assistant area, else from its name. Set up › on the Audio line in ⋯ opens it too, and ticking Audio with nothing matched opens it at once. Several players can share a room (a Control4 zone and a Denon zone, say): ▲ and ▼ set their order, and the first is the one the map's speaker follows. Every pick is kept the moment it's made (the card's guesses too, as it opens), so there is no Save: Done closes it. Someone who isn't an administrator sees the button greyed.

  • Which players: Control4, Crestron, Savant, Savant Audio, Russound, Nuvo, Monoprice, Xantech, Triad, Denon, Yamaha and Anthem, by the Home Assistant integration they come from. Include Home Assistant speakers on the matching card (off unless ticked) adds every other player with a volume: Music Assistant, ESPHome, Cast, TVs.

Audio needs PadSpan Pro or the Audio add-on. The ⋯ list is part of the new layout, PadSpan Pro's; on the classic layout, Settings → Atlas → Atlas — Mapped Light Control → Audio (off by default, shown where Audio is allowed) does the same for the whole house: the speaker beside a playing room's name, and the sheet's Audio with Match rooms.

Heatmap (Pro or the Climate map add-on)

Settings → Atlas → Atlas — Mapped Light Control → Heatmap chip on the Atlas (temperature, humidity, CO₂), off by default, adds a ♨ Heatmap chip to the Atlas. Tap it to step through Temperature, Humidity and CO₂ (the kinds your house has a sensor for), then off. Each room is coloured from its own sensors: the placed ones, or, with none placed, the average of the room's sensors at its centre. Nothing crosses a wall, a room with no sensor stays uncoloured, and a reading over an hour old isn't used.

Power teams

Smart colour bulbs in sockets fed by a wall dimmer or switch (Zigbee bulbs on a Control4 dimmer, say) go dark when someone turns the wall off, and come back in the wrong colour. A power team makes the dimmer and its bulbs one light in Home Assistant. Power teams are in the free edition: they need no PadSpan Pro key (PadSpan Bright has none, since it gives Home Assistant no entities).

Make one: Settings → Atlas → Power teams, + Add a team. Name it ("Bedroom lights"), pick Power from (the wall dimmer or switch, or None: these lights always have power for colour lights on their own supply, such as LED strings), tick Its bulbs, and Save. An administrator makes and changes teams. Each team is then a light in Home Assistant under its name (light.<team>, chapter 14): give it its room there, then place it on the map.

What it does

  • On puts the power on at full, sends the bulbs their brightness and colour again while they start, and says which bulb never answered.
  • Off does what you chose for the team: Turns the bulbs off, power stays on (they come back at once, in the right colour; new teams start on this) or Cuts the power.
  • The wall control still works. PadSpan sees the power come on and sets the bulbs. A dimmer held low at the wall sets how bright the bulbs go, while the dimmer itself goes back to full: smart bulbs need the whole wave.
  • Follow the day: cool and bright at midday, warm at sunrise and sunset, dim and warmer through the night, moved every minute with a slow fade. Choose a colour or brightness yourself and it stops until the team is next turned on, or until you choose Follow the day again. Sleep is one dim, warm setting. The curve's numbers (Brightest, by day, Dimmest, at midnight, Coolest, at noon, Warmest, at sunrise and sunset, Sleep: brightness, Sleep: colour) are yours to change.
  • A fade of up to 100 minutes, from an automation: while it fades, the day doesn't move it.

On the map, a power team is one thing: place the dimmer, one of its bulbs or the team's light. A tap switches the team, a drag dims it, and a hold opens its card. A dimmer left on with its bulbs off shows dark, as the room is. Show power teams on the settings card shows them in the list.

The team's card says what the team is doing (for example Off · the power is on, so it comes on at once, Power on: the bulbs are starting, On · set by hand, Waking up: fading in), with:

  • Follow the day and Sleep;
  • the wall dimmer's Power, with Cut power and Power on;
  • each bulb, with Try again for one that didn't answer;
  • A tap's off: what a tap on the map does when the team is on;
  • The day's light: when this light's day starts and ends: Sunrise moved by and Sunset moved by some minutes, or Day starts at a fixed time and Day ends at a fixed time (a bedroom whose day starts at 6:30 all year);
  • a wake-up: on the days and at the time you choose (Full light at, Fading in over), a team that is off comes on at its dimmest and warmest and fades to full by then. A team already on is left alone. Save the wake-up;
  • Advanced: the day's curve.

An administrator changes the wake-up and the curve. The sun curve is adapted from Adaptive Lighting.

Showcase, Automorph and presets (Pro)

Showcase, its themes, the West Coast Forest and the presets bar also come with the Looks pack add-on, on the free map; Whole house presets with the Vacation Mode & presets add-on.

  • Showcase: 20 themes for the whole map (colour, room edges, glow and light pools together). Pick one in the Atlas toolbar under Presentation.
  • Automorph: a soft glow drawn on the map from a fixture out into its room, in a choice of styles (toolbar → Automorph). It changes the map's look, not the lights.
  • The presets bar saves a whole look (theme, Automorph style, and if you like floor, spacing and layout) under a name, and Apply brings it back.
  • Whole house presets remembers the house itself: Set keeps every light's and fan's state (on or off, brightness, colour, effect, fan speed), and Apply puts the whole house back that way, after asking. Locks are never included.

Vacation Mode (Pro or the Vacation Mode & presets add-on)

The first entry in the Whole house presets list. It learns your house's routine from Home Assistant's history (each light, each day of the week) and keeps switching lights to match, with some randomness so it doesn't look like a timer, until you turn it off. While it runs, a banner sits in the middle of the Atlas with an Energy saving slider (100% follows the whole routine; lower lights less at once). Turning it on or off needs an administrator.

  • Turning it off asks first. Home Assistant then shows who turned it off, when and from where, and the same message goes to your Follow alert targets.
  • Turned back on within 3 weeks of a trip, it carries on with that trip's routine instead of learning from the days the house sat empty.
  • Options, each off until set: Only after dark, Ends (a date and time; it turns itself off and tells you), Pause while someone is home, Bedtime (lights go off from about then until 5 am, moving up to 20 minutes each night), Longest on.
  • binary_sensor.padspan_vacation_mode and the padspan_ha_vacation_mode event are in chapter 14. Restoring a PadSpan backup leaves it as it is.

Outdoor weather

When it rains or snows, faint rain or snow falls around the house on the Atlas, never over a room. PadSpan Pro or PadSpan Bright Pro animates it; the free map shows it still. Set it up in Settings → Atlas → Atlas — Mapped Light Control → Outdoor weather (Advanced mode): the switch (on by default), a rain sensor (optional), the weather entity, the warnings sensor (found by itself) and Strength (0.5× to 1.5×).

  • Light rain when it's wet: a rain sensor decides if you chose one, otherwise the weather entity.
  • Nothing when an on/off rain sensor reads wet but the weather entity says sunny, clear night or partly cloudy: dew and frost wet a rain sensor on clear mornings.
  • The weather entity's answer when the rain sensor hasn't reported for over 3 hours.
  • Heavy rain only on a rainfall warning, or when the weather entity says pouring.
  • Snow when the weather entity says snowy, or at 1 °C or colder. Heavy snow only on a snowfall warning.

Warnings are found by themselves from Environment Canada, MeteoAlarm, DWD and NINA, and Météo-France. In the USA, the weatheralerts or nws_alerts custom integrations add them.

Emergency lighting test

A round red Test emergency lighting button at the top right of the map, shown only when PadSpan finds emergency lights: a Home Assistant group with "emergency" in its name (nested groups too), or else WLED lights named "emergency". It needs PadSpan Pro or the Alarm & security add-on (a Bright Pro key keeps it); without either, there is no test, and the button shows only an alarm going off.

  • Tap the red centre: every emergency light comes on, and it says Test on — tap to end. Lights already on stay on when it ends.
  • Tap the ring for the list of emergency lights.
  • Force off turns every emergency light off, including those on before; it asks for a second tap.
  • While a Home Assistant automation with "emergency" in its name runs (a real emergency), the test won't switch those lights off under it.
  • To hide the button on a screen anyone can tap: Settings → Atlas → Atlas — Mapped Light Control → Show the Test emergency lighting button on the Atlas.
  • Your house alarm is inside the button when Home Assistant has an alarm panel: the centre becomes its shield in its colour, and the centre and the ring both open one card, the alarm on top (with PadSpan Pro, the Alarm & security add-on or Bright Pro, Arm and Disarm, always after a question and with the code when the alarm wants one) and the emergency lights below, with Start test. A house with an alarm and no emergency lights gets the alarm's own button, a plain ring. An alarm going off shows on the button on every edition. See chapter 13.

WLED: Advanced and Exact look (Pro or Bright Pro)

A WLED light's card has Controls | Advanced. Advanced is a whole WLED workbench in tabs: Layout, Effect, Presets & playlists, LEDs, Sync & team, Exact look, Settings, Backup and Info.

Exact look makes a WLED light come on looking the same every time:

  1. Set the light as you like it, then on Exact look choose Remember this look (or Remember team look for every light in its WLED sync team).
  2. On Sync & team, under Who gives this light its instructions, pick PadSpan (exact look) instead of WLED sync. WLED's own sync is switched off on that device, and put back if you switch back.

Only an administrator can change who runs a light.

Openers, locks and lights from relays (Pro)

Openers and locks also come with the Alarm & security add-on, as does ticking an opener on Devices → Door Openers; a light from relays stays Pro's.

A garage door, window opener, door strike or light wired to plain relays? Build an Opener, Lock, or Light from Relays (+ Build an opener, lock, or light from relays in the Atlas builder's list) makes it a proper Home Assistant cover, lock or light.

  • Opener: the open and close relays (two different ones), the travel time for a full open or close, and a position sensor if you have one (Invert if it reads backwards). Without a sensor, the position is remembered from the commands.
  • Lock: the relay, the unlock pulse and the auto re-lock time, in seconds.
  • Light: the relay and its pulse.

Build makes the script, automation and entity; Remove takes them away again.
