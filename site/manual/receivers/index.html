5. Receivers: which, how many, where

Contents

A receiver is anything that hears Bluetooth and passes what it hears to Home Assistant: an ESP32 board running ESPHome, a Shelly, the Home Assistant machine's own Bluetooth, an ESPresense node or a Tasmota device. Other software calls them Bluetooth proxies or scanners. PadSpan reads them all through Home Assistant, so a receiver you already have works with nothing to add.

How many

ReceiversWhat you get
One in the houseHome or away, not rooms. Everything it hears counts as being in its room.
One in each room you want told apartRooms. Each device goes to the room whose receiver hears it best. An open kitchen and living room count as one room unless each end has its own receiver.
Three or more placed on a floor planAlso positions between the receivers, so a device can be put in a room with no receiver of its own, like a closet or a hall.

You don't need to buy them all first. Start with what Home Assistant already hears. PadSpan shows a sample house until it hears the first receiver. Then add receivers where the scores in Calibration tell you to (see Finding the rooms that need one).

Which kind

You can mix them. Different radios hear the same phone at different strengths, so in a mixed house calibration matters more: it lets PadSpan allow for each receiver on its own.

ESPHome Bluetooth proxy (an ESP32 board)

The usual choice. Home Assistant finds an ESPHome Bluetooth proxy by itself, and PadSpan uses it as soon as Home Assistant does.

  • Pick a board with an external antenna: an ESP32-S3 with a small IPEX (u.FL) connector and a 2.4 GHz antenna. The antenna matters more than the chip. Boards with only the antenna printed on the board give readings too noisy to tell rooms apart: two rooms can be only a few dB apart, and a noisy antenna flips that call at random.
  • Wi-Fi is fine. Ethernet doesn't place devices noticeably better, though it suits a receiver that never moves.
  • Older ESP32 boards without an external antenna are good enough for home or away, not for rooms.

Shelly, Gen2 or newer

Shelly Plus, Pro, Gen3 and Gen4 devices on mains power can be receivers. Battery Shellys and Gen1 can't. Shelly BLU buttons and sensors are things a receiver hears, not receivers.

  1. In Home Assistant, open Settings → Devices & services → Shelly.
  2. Choose the cogwheel on the Shelly's entry.
  3. Set Bluetooth scanner mode to Auto (Active and Passive work too) and choose Submit.
  4. Home Assistant lists the Shelly's scanner as a device of its own, under Settings → Devices & services → Bluetooth. That device's area is the receiver's room. A scanner with no area of its own takes its Shelly's.

Most Shellys sit in a wall box or a fuse box with a small built-in antenna, so they hear less, and less steadily, than a receiver out in the room. Treat them as a free start: one is enough for home or away, and whether they are good enough for rooms depends on the house. Shellys can't hold a Bluetooth connection, which room presence doesn't need.

Home Assistant's own Bluetooth

The Home Assistant machine's built-in Bluetooth, or a USB Bluetooth adapter, is a receiver once Home Assistant's Bluetooth integration has it. It counts as one receiver in the room where the machine is. A machine in a cupboard or a basement hears that corner of the house.

ESPresense

ESPresense nodes report over MQTT instead of through Home Assistant's Bluetooth.

  1. Set up Home Assistant's MQTT integration.
  2. In PadSpan, open Manage → HA Entities → ESPresense MQTT and tick Enable ESPresense MQTT ingestion. Your nodes show up as receivers within about ten seconds.
  3. If your nodes publish under another topic than espresense, type it in Topic prefix: and choose Save.

The card is marked Experimental.

Tasmota (experimental)

An ESP32 running Tasmota with Bluetooth reports over MQTT too.

  1. Set up Home Assistant's MQTT integration.
  2. In PadSpan, open Manage → HA Entities → Tasmota BLE and tick Read Tasmota BLE over MQTT.
  3. On each Tasmota, type these in its console:
    • TelePeriod 10
    • BLEMaxAge 30
    • BLEAddrFilter 1

Tasmota's own settings are made for an occasional list of devices. Without these, it reports too slowly for rooms (a reading older than about 15 seconds doesn't count), keeps a device that left on its list, and never reports phones and tags that change address. PadSpan reads the topics tele/+/BLE and stat/+/BLE.

Passive receivers and Home Assistant 2026.4

Since Home Assistant 2026.4, repeat readings from passive receivers (Shellys and others that only listen) can be held back. PadSpan fetches them again on a timer: Settings → Tracking → Receivers → Bluetooth refresh interval, 5 seconds on a new install. If a Shelly hears devices but shows no signal during calibration, check that interval is 5 seconds, or tick Refresh every 5 s for passive proxy scanners on the same card, which keeps it at 5 seconds even when the interval is longer. It uses slightly more processor time.

Where to put them

  • Inside the rooms you want told apart, not in halls.
  • Nearer the middle of a room than a corner. Not behind large metal things, not inside a cabinet, and not against an outside wall.
  • Close enough that next-door receivers overlap a little.
  • In a large room one receiver is the least; two do noticeably better.
  • About head height or higher. Then set the real height (below).

A receiver's room, place and height

Room. A receiver's room is the Home Assistant area of its device. A receiver with no area gives no room, however well it hears you: a phone right next to it lands in the next receiver's room. The Overview's Who's where now card lists every receiver without one and lets you set it there: chapter 4. Rooms and areas are explained in chapter 8. If you move a receiver to another room, change its area too.

Place. For positions between receivers, a receiver also needs a spot on your floor plan: chapter 9, step 5. Place it where it really is.

Height. Set its height above its own floor in Calibration → Tune (Height above floor (m):, which starts at 2.4 m), in Mapping's receivers step, or in the Height column of the Receivers screen. All three are the same value. A height can be set only once the receiver has a place on a floor plan.

Signal adjustment. Some receivers read weaker than others at the same distance. Settings → Tracking → Receivers → Receiver signal adjustments lists each receiver with a box from −20 to +20 dB, added to every reading from it (positive if it reads weaker than it should). The figure beside each box is only a suggestion: it is not applied until you type it in and Save. Most homes leave these at 0 and calibrate instead.

Seeing how your receivers are doing

  • Receivers (in the menu): every receiver in one table, with its Room, Floor, On a floor plan, Height, Signal adjustment, Status, Devices now and Last heard. Problems only shows just the ones that need you. Place opens Mapping with the receiver picked.
  • The status dot in the top bar: a receiver is Silent when nothing has come from it for over 10 minutes.
  • Health → Hearing less than usual: shown only when a receiver has heard far fewer Bluetooth messages than normal for over 10 minutes while the others are fine. Rooms near it can be wrong. Restarting it often helps.
  • Health → Scanner Health: for each receiver, how often it agrees with the room the others point to. The Reliability figure is only shown: a low one doesn't make that receiver count for less.
  • Health → Scanner → Room Mappings: every receiver by its Bluetooth address, with its room.

A receiver that is silent: chapter 26.

Finding the rooms that need one

  • Calibration → Model shows how often PadSpan picks the right room on spots it wasn't calibrated on. A room that keeps scoring badly is the first place to add or move a receiver.
  • Calibration → Accuracy by spot shows which spots go wrong, and to which room.
  • Calibration → Roam shows where you have calibrated so far and where to stand next.
  • Walk a room, when it is on, shows a room's score before and after a 90-second walk.

All of these are in chapter 11.

Next to Bermuda

Already running Bermuda? Keep it. PadSpan reads the same Home Assistant Bluetooth data Bermuda does, so your ESPHome proxies and Shelly scanners serve both at once.

  • Bermuda stays as it is. Installing, reloading or removing PadSpan doesn't change Bermuda's devices, entities or settings, and a test in PadSpan's code fails if new code tries to.
  • Your Bermuda devices show up in PadSpan as devices of their own. If you would rather PadSpan used only its own readings, tick Ignore Bermuda (Ignore all Bermuda integration data) in Settings → Tracking → Home Assistant and integrations.
  • Phones you set up for Bermuda keep working. An iPhone or Apple Watch in Home Assistant's Private BLE Device is recognised by the same key and named as you named it there: chapter 6.
  • With one receiver per room, PadSpan picks rooms by which receiver hears a device best, as Bermuda does: expect rooms about as good, and a calibration walk to correct some calls. With three or more receivers on a measured floor plan, it can also place a device between them.

Your automations can stay on Bermuda's sensors until you decide to change them. PadSpan's own sensors are in chapter 14.
