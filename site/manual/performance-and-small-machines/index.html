23. Performance and small machines

Contents

What PadSpan costs the Home Assistant machine and the screens, and what to turn on when either struggles. The short version of the requirements is in chapter 2.

The Home Assistant machine

RecommendedMore than 2 GB of memory: a Raspberry Pi 4 or 5 with 4 GB or more, a Home Assistant Green, or a mini PC or virtual machine with 4 GB or more.
2 GB machinesNot recommended for now. If you have one, turn on Low-memory mode (below).
StorageAn SSD or eMMC rather than an SD card. The device history is saved up to every 15 seconds: in a house with about 2,900 devices that file is about 5 MB, up to about 30 GB of writes a day.

Why a busy street costs memory

PadSpan keeps every device it hears for a while, not only the ones you follow. Three things grow with them:

  • The device history: every device heard that isn't a phone's changing address, kept for a day by default (Settings → Tracking → What the panel shows → Object History Retention: 1, 2, 7 or 14 days), at most 10,000 that nobody has named. Named and followed devices are always kept.
  • The Bluetooth cache: the last reading from every receiver for every address, kept for the BLE Advertisement Timeout (same section; 4 hours unless you change it).
  • The live picture: all of that built into one list for every open PadSpan screen, every 5 seconds.

In a quiet house that is a few hundred devices. Next to a busy street or in a block of flats it can be tens of thousands; one house reported over 80,000.

Measured on 64-bit Python with six receivers (real use runs 10 to 30% higher):

Devices heldPadSpan's memory (held / peak with a screen open)
1,000about 14 / 20 MB
5,000about 67 / 100 MB
25,000about 340 / 500 MB
80,000about 1.0 / 1.6 GB

A 2 GB Raspberry Pi has about 0.4 to 0.8 GB left for PadSpan once Home Assistant OS and a few add-ons run, so it fits only about 5,000 devices.

Low-memory mode

Settings → Tracking → Performance → Low-memory mode, off by default. For a 2 GB machine: it caps how many passing devices PadSpan holds.

NormallyWith Low-memory mode
How long the Bluetooth cache keeps an addressThe BLE Advertisement Timeout (4 hours unless changed)15 minutes at most
Addresses in the Bluetooth cacheNo limit2,000
Unnamed devices in the device history10,000, for 1 to 14 days2,000, for 1 day
Adverts in the live picture5,0002,000

Together that is about 4,000 devices at most. It changes nothing else: named, labelled and followed devices are never dropped, and rooms, positions, the Atlas and Live Aboard work as before. Passing strangers just drop out of the lists sooner. The card asks before it turns on.

The other Performance settings

All in Settings → Tracking → Performance:

  • Presence Poll Interval (5 seconds): how often rooms are worked out. Higher is lighter on the processor and slower to follow you.
  • CPU Mode: where that work runs. Shared (the default) runs it with Home Assistant: fastest, and it competes with Home Assistant. Single core holds it to one core so Home Assistant stays responsive. Dedicated core gives it a core of its own. It applies from the next update, no restart.
  • Optimize for scale (off): for several screens at once. The Overview and the 3D people get only what they draw (about 50 KB a screen instead of about 2 MB in a busy house), screens asking at the same moment share one build, and a hidden screen asks for nothing. Details in chapter 18.

On Settings → Tracking → Receivers, a longer Bluetooth refresh interval reads the receivers less often (but keep 5 seconds for passive ones such as Shellys: chapter 5).

The screens

The panel and the AtlasAny current browser: Chrome, Edge, Safari or Firefox, on a computer, tablet or phone. A tablet that runs Home Assistant's dashboards smoothly runs the Atlas.
Live Aboard (the 3D house)A browser with WebGL 2. Without it, or on a graphics chip too slow to keep up, PadSpan shows the flat Atlas instead and says so.

For a slow screen:

  • set Live Aboard's Quality to Low (Settings → Atlas → Live Aboard). On Auto it drops by itself when the screen can't keep up;
  • Show the frame rate (this browser only), on the same card, shows how many times a second the house is drawn;
  • the flat Atlas wall (/padspan-lights?kiosk=1) asks Home Assistant for the least.

With nothing changing, Live Aboard draws nothing, so an idle wall costs almost nothing.

When Home Assistant is slow or runs out of memory

  1. Turn on Low-memory mode.
  2. Shorten Object History Retention to 1 day, and the BLE Advertisement Timeout.
  3. With several wall screens, turn on Optimize for scale.
  4. Try CPU Mode → Single core.

More, by symptom: chapter 26.

How these numbers are gathered

Only from installs that turned on Settings → Tracking → Help improve PadSpan: what PadSpan holds in memory, how fast new devices arrive, how often Home Assistant restarted, and how hard Home Assistant and each screen work. Counts and bands only (chapter 24). Guidance for screens, by kind of device, will follow once there is enough of it.
