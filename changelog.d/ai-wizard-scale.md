# Let Claude draw your rooms: the plan's size, read off the plan

### New
- **A plan with no scale can use Let Claude draw your rooms (experimental).** Before, the helper needed a plan whose scale was already set. Now Claude first looks for a measurement printed on your plan (usually under a minute; you can leave the page and come back). It shows the plan with the measurement drawn in amber between the marks at its ends, a close look at the printed text, and asks: "Claude read **30′-5½″** here, across the whole house (9.28 m). Is that what your plan says?" Press **Yes, that's right**, or **No, it says:** and type what it says (feet and inches, or metres; the helper shows what it understood), or **Measure something myself instead**. When the other direction was read too, it says so ("and down the side: 24′-0″ (7.32 m) ✓"). The footer says how big that makes your plan, and "Save and find the rooms" goes straight on to Claude's rooms read.
- **No measurement printed, or none Claude could read:** the helper asks you to measure one thing instead: click both ends of something you know the length of (a wall, a doorway, the whole house) and type its length, such as 4.2 m, 420 cm or 13' 9". It shows what it understood ("= 4.19 m", and for a number typed with no unit which one it took: "= 12.00 m (read as metres)", "= 0.45 m (read as millimetres)"). Two taps put the two ends; drag an end, or tap near it, to move it. It works with a finger too.
- **It is saved exactly as Mapping → Edit → Measure saves a measurement**, through the same code, from the two ends and the length (as Claude read it, or as you typed it): PadSpan works the scale out itself. The house's metre scale is set from it only if your house has none yet. A plan that already has a scale skips this step, and nothing about it changes.
- **Which floor:** asked only when the plan's floor isn't one of your house's floors.
- **Information and a choice, never a stop.** "Across and down disagree by 6 %: the picture may be stretched. The average is used, as Mapping's Measure does." "That makes your plan 412 m across. Is the number right?" You can change the number or save it as it is.
- **Reads:** with no scale yet, about five reads for one plan (up to three to find the printed measurement, two for the rooms). Claude's first look at the plan is kept, so a later Line up plans of the same picture doesn't read it again.
- **Undo** on the last screen takes the rooms back first, then the plan's scale the helper saved, so the plan has no scale again, as long as nothing in metres changed since (a room, a wall, a receiver, a zone, something in 3D, a calibration spot, even one PadSpan records by itself, or another plan's scale). If something was, the scale stays and the helper says so; change it in Mapping → Edit → Measure.

### Changed
- **The helper stops only to keep your data safe.** It won't save a scale for a plan that already has one or has calibration spots, a plan whose picture changed while you were on the screen or has no size on record, a plan measured in Mapping → Edit → Measure while it was saving, or a plan whose floor it doesn't know; and it saves nothing if the safety backup can't be made. A backup of your map, plans and calibration is taken before each save and each undo (the last six kept). A save cut short by a restart is finished or taken back the next time the helper starts.
- **If Claude's reading stops** (out of credit, can't be reached, stopped part-way, ten readings a day), the helper says why with the same words and buttons as the rooms read; what was read is kept.
- **Usage report (if you have it turned on):** how a plan's scale was found (a printed measurement Claude read, none found, measured by you, or set already), what you did with Claude's reading (yes, a corrected number and how far it was from Claude's, or measured yourself instead), how many directions were read and how far across and down disagreed, how each scale save and undo ended, the scale read's runs and reads alongside the line-up's, and, if you later measure a plan again by hand in Mapping → Edit → Measure, by how much the scale moved (under 1 %, 1–3 %, 3–10 %, over 10 %). Counts and fixed words only, never a plan, a length or a floor.
